The film

The stakes climb rather than repeat: brand new, then rented, then a certainty, then a long day in the good thing, then entirely her own choice. Every one of them is a moment anybody would recognise instantly, which is the whole test. If you have to explain why it is brave, it is the wrong frame.

Be brave.

Same close as the first film. Four of them are what other people say to you. The last one is what we say.

Why this one exists. White is a visual risk and it runs out fast, because there are only so many ways to film a pale shirt. The terms of the garment are an emotional risk and they never run out, because everybody owns something they will not wear for a reason that has nothing to do with its colour. Rented, dry clean only, brand new, a wedding that is only halfway through, or just pale on the wrong night. Same format, same stamp, endless material.
Same voice as the first film, and it stays flat. Six hours in is the detail that makes the wedding one land, because she has already got that far, and it is funnier the less it is performed.
Nothing gets spilled and nothing gets stained. Not a drip, not a mark. The whole film lives in the second before, and there is no product in it.
Nothing in here can imply the product on a fabric it may be too harsh for. This rule cost three frames. A vintage dress and an inherited coat both went, because a film that says there isn't another one is quietly promising the thing can be saved, and those are exactly the fabrics where it might not be. By the same logic the suede coat in frame 1 became wool, since suede is a worse candidate than vintage. No vintage, no heirlooms, no suede, no silk. Every garment in this film is a normal modern washable or dry cleanable thing.

The feel, same as the first film

Watch it. The words here are doing far less work than the look is. It needs to be beautiful and it needs to look expensive. That is the whole reference.

Reference · the texture

Heels carried in the lift, brass taps, earrings at the mirror. Expensive, and everybody in it looks beautiful. That is the bar. The good clothes are treated as a character rather than a prop, the light does most of the work, and it holds every shot well past the point a reel normally cuts.
