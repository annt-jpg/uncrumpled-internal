Concept 1 · Film plan · Video 4

Top 3 things to pack

ListicleProduct nestedTravel
Format
Listicle to camera or hands only, filmed against the open case. 15 to 20 sec.
The concept
Three things you always pack. Uncrumpled sits at position two, never at the end and never as the reveal. The other two items are real things you actually pack, so the list is not a set up for the product.
How to shoot it

Open case, already packed, on the bed or a bench.

Item one out of the case, held up, named. Something unglamorous and real.

Item two, the travel bottle. Say what it does in one line and move on. Do not linger.

Item three. Something that gets a reaction on its own.

Zip the case. End there. No pitch.

Do not let the bottle be the punchline. If the edit lands on it, the format is broken. No timing claims on the one line about what it does.

Concept 5 · New this session

The timed demo, the spray

ProofOne unbroken takeReal stopwatch in frame
Format
Locked-off single take, no cuts, a phone or a laptop propped in shot running its own stopwatch. 20 to 30 sec.
The concept
The clock is a real device in the frame, not a graphic added later. Phone leaning against something, or a laptop open on the bench, stopwatch app running. Camera does not move and the take does not cut. Start the stopwatch on camera at zero, crumpled shirt, spray, smooth, hold it up. The stopwatch is still running when the shot ends. Whatever the real duration turns out to be is what it says.
How to shoot it

Tripod, locked off. Do not touch it once the take starts.

Prop the phone or the laptop where the screen is readable but not the subject of the shot. Off to one side, in focus, small in frame.

Even light edge to edge across the garment and across the screen. Uneven light reads as a trick even when nothing was faked, and a blown-out screen is unreadable.

Start rolling, then tap the stopwatch on camera. Your hand starting it is what proves it started at zero.

Spray, smooth, hold it up. One continuous move.

Let the stopwatch keep running past the reveal. Do not stop it, do not cut on a round number.

THIS ONE HAS A RULE COLLISION, READ IT. Timing claims are hard-banned in Uncrumpled copy: "in seconds" was stripped from every ad on 13 Jul, and both Video 6 and the four-minute concept say show the effort, never put a number on it. A stopwatch running on a real device in frame is footage of what happened rather than a claim, which is why it survives at all, and a real device is a stronger version of that than a burned-in graphic because a graphic is exactly the thing an edit can fake. To keep it that way: no number in the headline, no number in the primary text, no number as on-screen text, and no target time. The stopwatch screen is the only place a number appears anywhere in the ad.
Concept 5b · New this session

The timed demo, the iron

ProofOne unbroken takeReal stopwatch in frame
Format
Identical build to Concept 5. Locked-off single take, no cuts, same phone or laptop stopwatch in shot. Runs as long as it runs.
The concept
Same shot, same shirt, the iron instead. Stopwatch starts before you have touched anything, so it covers the whole job and not just the pressing: fetch the iron, set up the board, fill it, wait for it to heat, press, hang it up, put it all away. Nothing is said and nothing is sped up. The length of the take is the entire point, which is why it must not be cut down.
How to shoot it

Same camera position, same light, same device in the same spot as Concept 5. Shot for shot identical, or the pair does not read.

Start the stopwatch on camera before you move. The setup and the pack down are the argument, not the pressing.

Do the whole job at a normal, unhurried pace. Do not perform frustration and do not dawdle. A staged struggle kills it.

Keep rolling through the pack down. Board folded, iron cooled and away, back to where you started.

Do not cut. If it is too long to run whole, this is a slow-cut long-form asset, not a shortened one.

DECIDE HOW THE PAIR RUNS BEFORE YOU SHOOT IT. Two separate ads, each honest on its own, is fine. Cutting them together as a race with clocks on both is a timing comparison, which is the thing the timing-claim ban exists to stop, and split-screen Type B versus Type C was killed outright on 1 May. It also collides with the half-and-half verdict from 14 Aug: the DEVICE of making the effort gap visible is kept, that specific execution is burned. Safest run is the iron take on its own, no spray in it at all, and let the viewer bring the other one. Second thing: the honest bit is that the shirt still has to be washed either way, so do not imply the iron take is a job the spray removes forever, only that it is the longer one today.
Concept 6 · New this session

10,000 orders

To cameraFounderSocial proof in one object
Format
To camera, holding the thing up. Short. 20 to 30 sec. Shoot it a little unpolished.
The concept
You hold up the trophy and say what it is for. Not a flex and not a thank-you video. It is one object that tells a stranger other people already bought this, which is the job a review normally does.
How to shoot it

Hold it up first, before you explain it. The object earns the three seconds.

Say what it is and what it is for, in one line.

One line on where it started. Spray bottle, a hatred of ironing, no idea anyone else would care.

One line on what it actually means, which is people, not a number.

Stop. No CTA. Let Tail C carry it, or nothing at all.

TWO THINGS TO CONFIRM BEFORE THIS ROLLS. First, the order count still contradicts itself in our own scripts: 10,000 in seven months in one place, 13,000 in eight months in another. It has been flagged twice and never settled. You say the number out loud in this one, so it cannot be fixed in the edit. Pick one and stick to it across the whole shoot. Second, confirm what the trophy actually is and whether it names a figure on it, because if the object says one number and you say another on camera the ad is unusable.
Concept 7 · Ad concepts · AD3

Six steps, one shirt

Counted listOne shot per stepSound off
Format
Counted list, one shot per step, fast cut. 12 sec.
The concept
Board out, unfold, plug in, wait, press, wait, fold, put away. Number each step on screen as it happens. Then one shot at the end: spray, smooth. No number on that one.
How to shoot it

Board comes out of wherever it lives. Number 1.

Unfold it, set the legs. Number 2.

Find the cord, plug it in. Number 3.

Wait for the light. Hold this shot longer than is comfortable. Number 4.

Press. Number 5.

Fold it, put it away, walk it back to where it lives. Number 6.

Hard cut. Spray, smooth. No number appears.

The waiting shots are the ad. If the edit trims them to keep it snappy the argument disappears. No timing claim on screen or in copy, the count does that job instead.
Concept 8 · Swipe file 14 Aug · E3 TUSHY

This one is for you

One setupFive openersVolume engine
Format
One body, filmed once. Five different first lines recorded separately and cut onto the front. 12 to 15 sec each.
The concept
Write the whole ad for one narrow group and lose everybody else in the first line, on purpose. TUSHY run three of these at once, one for people with IBS, one for women on GLP-1s, one for flare days, and nobody else is addressed at any point in any of them.
How to shoot it

Film the BODY once, and never let it mention who it is for. “This is what I use instead. Spray it, smooth it with your hand, put it on. No board, no cord, nothing to plug in.”

Then record the five openers separately, straight to camera, one take each.

Opener 1, Billy: “If you are a flight attendant, this one is for you.”

Opener 2, Jas: “If you get changed at work, this is for you.”

Opener 3, Jas: “If you are back at your desk by two and the shirt has already gone, this is for you.”

Opener 4, Mia: “If you are the one who irons somebody else’s work shirts, this is for you.”

Opener 5, Sienna: “If you have booked the flights and you are already thinking about what you can pack, this is for you.”

Clusters, body plus hooks

Longest job on the day, so it goes last. Six persona-led clusters. Each one is a single body plus its own set of three to five second openers, so one body gives you up to six ads. Hooks never move between clusters and a body never restates its own hook.
Cluster 1 · body

Gets changed at work

Persona

Alex, the office professional. Corporate or client-facing, gets dressed somewhere that is not home.

Angle

The shirt was fine when you packed it. It is not fine when it comes out, there is no iron wherever you are, and now you are late as well.

What has to match between the hooks and this body
The demo has to be on a shirt pulled straight out of a bag with hard fold lines across it. Not a generically scrunched shirt. Every hook promises the bag moment, so the body has to show the bag moment.
Body script

You go to get changed. You pull your shirt out of your bag and it has that fold line straight across the middle, or it is scrunched to no end.

So now you either wear it like that, or you go looking for an iron that is not going to be there.

And now you are fifteen minutes late on top of it.

(pull the shirt from the bag, spray, smooth, hold it up)

This lives in my bag. Non-toxic, and it washes out in the next wash.

Cluster 2 · body

Get the twenty minutes back

Persona

Mia, the working mum, and anyone who is up earlier than they need to be. Core persona and the widest audience in the account.

Angle

Ironing time is sleep you are giving away. Stop doing it and you get the time back, so the sell is the extra twenty minutes, not the stress.

What has to match between the hooks and this body
The morning in this body has to look calm, not frantic. Every hook promises more sleep and a later start, so a chaotic rushing body would contradict the offer. Later alarm, unhurried, out the door on time.
Body script

I used to be up before everyone else just to stand at an ironing board.

Now I get up when I actually need to get up, and this is the entire reason why.

(spray, smooth, put it on, unhurried)

Dressed, out, and nobody knows the difference.

Non-toxic, and it washes out next wash.

Cluster 4 · body

Who actually wants to iron

Persona

Everyone. No job title, no life stage. The widest net in the shoot, built on the one thing the whole audience already agrees on.

The body cannot be set in a specific scenario, because the hooks are all general. Keep it to camera, keep it light. The second you put it in an office or a suitcase, the general hooks stop fitting.

Who actually wants to spend their time ironing?

Cluster 5 · body

You think this does not work

Persona

Not a life stage, a state of mind. Someone who has bought a wrinkle spray that did nothing, or assumes all of them are water in a bottle.

Angle

Do not argue. Offer the proof, then explain the mechanism while the demo is happening in front of them.

What has to match between the hooks and this body
Every hook here is a doubt or a challenge, so the body answers with proof first and the explanation running underneath it. A warm lifestyle hook on the front of this body wastes it.
Body script

Want proof, in real time? If you are sceptical, watch this.

(start the demo here, and keep talking over it)

When fabric folds, hydrogen bonds form inside the fibres and hold the crease in place.

Our proprietary CreaseDrop technology penetrates those bonds and relaxes them, releasing the fibres so the crease drops out, leaving the fabric Uncrumpled.

All without heat, or that damp, sprayed-on feeling.

(if you are shooting the half and half version: spray the left half only, lay it flat, one unbroken pan across both halves)

It will not do this on polyester, and I would rather tell you now than have you find out.

Then
Tail B, thirty day money back.

Cluster 6 · body

Why it exists at all

Persona

Cold audience who has never heard of you. The founder story is doing the work a review would normally do.

Angle

Someone was so unwilling to iron that building an entire company was the more appealing option. And then she kept going until it actually worked.

What has to match between the hooks and this body
All three hooks are jokes at your own expense, so the body has to start light and then earn it. If the body opens serious, the tonal drop off the hook is obvious.
Body script

In my defence, I did try everything that was already out there first. All of it was useless, or it was as much work as just ironing.

Then my dad was diagnosed with cancer, and that changed what I was willing to put on my clothes.

So I reformulated the whole thing to be non-toxic.

Sixteen versions later I had it. (demo cutaway)

Now it ships all over Australia, which still surprises me.

Then
Tail C, silent, or Tail B.
