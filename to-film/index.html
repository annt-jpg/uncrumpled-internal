The two you asked for · 2 cards

TF 1

Standing in it, crumpled, then uncrumpled

Ann to cameraNo dialogueOne wall, one sitting
Two of the best performing ads this brand has ever run, put together.
Reference
Reference clip to come.
The idea
Stands straight on in a genuinely crumpled outfit, sprays it, creases drop while she is still in it. Cut. Next outfit, same frame. Six to eight, cut to five.
How to shoot it

Floor tape. Camera does not move between outfits. The repeat is the format.

Ball every garment the night before and sit on it. Never iron creases in.

One sitting so the light matches. Daylight, side on.

One continuous take each. No cut between spray and drop.

Open on the worst looking one, not the prettiest.

(grab the before and after stills while the wall is up. That is B 3 for free.)

Garment choice decides whether it works
  • Chambray, denim, cotton shirting. Proven, and the wrinkle reads from across a room.
  • Viscose or rayon slip. Drapes, so the drop is obvious.
  • Linen once only. It sells the angle, and it is also the biggest complaint cluster in the reviews.
  • No polyester. Real limit.
  • No silk, vintage, inherited or suede.
Props
6 to 8 garments balled overnight. Bottle. Tripod, floor tape, plain wall.
TF 2

Baby wipes against Unstain

No talkingNeeds drying timeSet up mid day
Here for the free second version below.
The idea
Identical shirts, identical stain, one method each, same framing. Napkin, water, a sleeve, club soda, a baby wipe. Then ours.
How to shoot it

Stain every shirt identically. Same food, amount, spot, height.

One method per shirt, same framing.

Then WAIT and film them dry. The ring is the argument. Wet, they all look like they worked.

Set up mid day, shoot something else while it dries, come back.

Run it again on a second food. Two versions, one setup.

(test off camera first. If it is not obvious on your phone, change the stain.)

Free second video, and it is for the product actually selling
  • Same build for the SPRAY. Objection is I will just hang it in the shower.
  • Four garments, same crease, same frame: steamy bathroom, damp hands, dryer for ten minutes, ours.
  • Film them dry. A shower-damp shirt looks smooth for about a minute.
  • Do the spray version first. Unstain has not launched yet.
Props
9 identical white shirts. Baby wipes, napkins, club soda, water. Two staining foods. Unstain, bottle.
Say baby wipes, never a brand name. A category is fair comment. A named product shown failing is a performance claim about someone else’s goods. Same for the dryer and the shower versions, describe the method, never a brand.

The ingredients video · 1 card, 5 ingredients

ING

Going through the ingredients, one at a time

To cameraFive parts, one sittingStatics fall out of it
The only script with published research behind every line. Answers the question the comments ask most: what is actually in it.
The idea
Table, the bottle, good light, sat down. One ingredient at a time. She says what it does, then what the report says, and moves on. No music, no graphics, nothing clever. Five short parts filmed in one sitting so any one of them can run on its own.
Script

Ann: People ask what is actually in this. So here it is, all five things.

(picks up the bottle)

Ann: Most of it is water. That is what gets into the fibre so the crease can drop. No heat.

Ann: Then the one that does the work. It relaxes what is holding the crease and leaves nothing stiff behind.

Ann: One that spreads the liquid out so you do not get wet patches.

Ann: A preservative, so it keeps. That one does nothing to your clothes, it just protects what is in the bottle.

Ann: And one that takes the smell out rather than covering it.

Ann: Three of those five have been assessed as safe for skin by an independent panel. I will put the reports in the comments.

Ann: It does not work on polyester. Never has.

How to shoot it

Film each ingredient as its own take with a clean start and a clean finish, so any one of them is a standalone ad.

Hold the bottle for the water line and put it down after. Do not hold it for the whole video or it becomes an infomercial.

Say the number, not an adjective. The real figure beats the word gentle every time.

Read the ingredient section below before filming. Two of the five have limits on what can be said and one of them has no study at all.

(while you are sat there, shoot the stills: the bottle on the table, one hand holding it, and a straight-on frame with room for text. That is the ingredient static set.)

Each one also works alone, opening on the question
  • "What is the preservative in it, and is that bad?" Then just card 04.
  • "Is there silicone in this?" Then just card 02, and answer it straight.
  • "Why does it not work on polyester?" The honest limit as its own ad.
  • "What is actually in it?" All five, under thirty seconds, fast.
Props
The bottle, a plain table, daylight. Nothing else.
Say assessed as safe by the Cosmetic Ingredient Review panel. It is true, checkable, and stronger than any adjective.
