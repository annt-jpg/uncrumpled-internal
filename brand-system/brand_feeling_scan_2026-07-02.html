How simple products
become feelings.

Six brands that sell something boring, then wrap it in a story and an emotion so strong you'd tattoo the logo. Each one goes deep across six lenses, with real Instagram data, colour palettes and links. Then every lesson is brought home to Uncrumpled.

How to read this

The six lenses.

Every brand is walked through the same six. Where a lens doesn't really apply, it's greyed and we say so.

1 · Villain

Do they make the boring old way (or an enemy) the bad guy?

2 · Emotion + community

Do they sell a feeling and a tribe, not the product?

3 · Guilt → pride

Do they flip guilt or shame into permission and pride?

4 · Branding

What language and verbs, and what visual look, do they own?

5 · Build in public

Is the founder or the journey the content?

6 · Entertainment

Is the content a bit worth sharing, not an ad?

Deep dive 01
Liquid Death
Canned water, tea & energy
Website ↗@liquiddeath ↗

Positioning: against smug, precious, wellness-cliché water branding. The enemy is corporate seriousness itself. They sell healthy products (water, tea, low-sugar sparkling, clean energy) wrapped in heavy-metal death comedy so "healthy" never feels self-righteous. Founder Mike Cessario: "an entertainment company that monetises via beverage."

Custom blackletter/gothic wordmark that reads like a death-metal band logo. Black-and-white core with vivid flavour-can accents, horror-comic imagery (skulls, severed hands, "Killbert" the grape). The masterstroke: the beer-style tallboy can, so water looks like something you'd hold at a concert, not on a yoga mat. (*accents approx.)

Signature formats (from their IG)
  • Fake-corporate "solves a problem" spot — deadpan press-release voice, e.g. Sparkling Energy "exploding heads from extreme energy drinks" (389K views).
  • Villain-flavour launch + mascot — "Killbert Grape"; "sCREAM Soda… so your organs will be good enough to be harvested for influencer marketing" (112K).
  • Absurd collab / stunt — Pop-Tarts Carnage Iced Tea on the Sphere; the Taylor Morrison "Killer House" giveaway.
  • Innuendo one-liner over product — "Size matters" (13.9M views, 822K likes).
  • Real-person "influencer" bit — hiring The Boys' fictional VP "as their latest influencer" (1.3M views).
The six lenses
1 · Villain

Corporate blandness, plastic pollution and "extreme" macho energy-drink culture, attacked by out-absurding all three ("Death to Plastic"). They never name one rival, they mock the whole self-serious health-marketing playbook, so every competitor is implicated at once.

2 · Emotion + community

A cult, not a customer base. Belonging through shared irreverence, monetised literally via the "Sell Your Soul" club (225,000+ people signed a legal contract). They turn hater comments into "Greatest Hates" death-metal vinyl, weaponising negativity into community.

3 · Guilt → pride

Flips health guilt (choosing water = opting out of fun) into water being the coolest thing you can hold. Also parent guilt: "official energy drink sponsor of the world's most extreme sport: parenting" (543K views) reframes exhausted parenting as hardcore.

4 · Branding

Owns "Murder your thirst," "Death to Plastic," "Sell your soul," and a fake-corporate-deadpan-spliced-with-gore voice. Product names are a system (Killbert, sCREAM, Cherry Obituary). The gothic wordmark + tallboy can make a post unmistakably them with no logo needed.

5 · Build in publicbarely

Not a founder journey. The nearest analogue is transparency-as-comedy: they narrate their own marketing machinery as the joke ("our latest influencer," "we ran a Big Game commercial to solve a Big Problem").

6 · Entertainment

The core engine, and the data proves it: the "Size matters" reel hit 13.9M views / 822K likes / 11K comments while a straight Prime Day discount post managed 293 likes. That ~2,800x gap is the whole thesis, comedy scales, coupons don't.

Deep dive 02
Graza
Extra virgin olive oil
Website ↗@getgraza ↗

Positioning: "very fancy oil in a decidedly un-fancy format." The enemy is the whole category convention, dark glass bottles, acidity percentages, region snobbery and prestige pricing that intimidate people into hoarding good oil. Names encode the anti-fussy stance: behaviour-based Sizzle / Drizzle / Frizzle instead of specs.

Palette & identity
#1F3B24*
#3E5F2E*
#8FD14F*
#E4F03A*
#F3D9B8*

Tonal greens on warm peachy-sand, deliberately fun not sophisticated. A drippy Spanish-inspired logotype + Garamond Condensed + typewriter type, with hand-drawn warm illustrations (the olive-with-a-spigot, the oil-flinging woman). The recyclable green squeeze bottle IS the logo. (*approx, not exposed in site CSS.)

Signature formats (from their IG)
  • Recipe / food-porn reels — a dish built around the product, all-caps energy (Crispy Pepperoni & Pecorino Chip Stack).
  • Product-drop carousels — new-SKU launches that rack up comments (4-flavour chips = 406 comments; Mayo & Aioli = 491).
  • IRL activation posts — the Food Network "Great Food Truck Tour," free food, city by city.
  • One-line shitpost meme — bare product shot + throwaway caption ("Correct answers only 🌭").
  • Cryptic viral teaser — "Time to go touch some glass!!!" (904K views, 1,803 comments).
The six lenses
1 · Villain

The pretentious olive-oil establishment and the behaviour it produces, good oil hoarded because it feels too precious to waste. Attacked structurally, not by naming rivals: the squeeze bottle mocks dark glass, the -izzle names mock acidity jargon. The Mayo launch even reframes against "Big Mayo" ("it's usually 65% oil, so make it with good oil").

2 · Emotion + community

Delight and in-crowd belonging, in a hyped-friend all-caps voice ("THEY'RE HERE!!!"). Community is farmed through comment-driven drops and real-world gatherings (the food-truck tour turns followers into a physical queue). Drops pull 400+ comments vs ~30 on a recipe, news and access mobilise the fanbase.

3 · Guilt → pride

Dissolves the "I'm wasting my nice oil" hoarding guilt and installs the pride of using premium EVOO daily and generously. "Made for eating, never heating," "Use it every day, in every way." They pour good oil on chips and hot dogs with zero reverence, the point is good oil is for using hard, not saving.

4 · Branding

Owns the -izzle system (Sizzle / Drizzle / Frizzle) so the name is the instruction, plus a voice of ALL-CAPS excitement, parenthetical asides ("(duh)") and folksy contractions. The green squeeze bottle silhouette is recognisable shelf-out, that bottle is the logo.

5 · Build in public

Muted on-feed, strong in founder Andrew Benin's press (named after a Spanish village; the bottle idea came from a Dr. Bronner's soap bottle). The grid narrates expansion in real time ("now rolling out at Costco in WA & OR").

6 · Entertainment

Dominant. The single biggest post is a pure attention play, "Time to go touch some glass" (904K views, 1,803 comments), dwarfing every recipe and launch. The tone is deadpan-hype and internet-native, closer to a shitposting meme account than a food brand, which is exactly what makes it shareable.

Deep dive 03
Hot Girl Pickles
Good Girl Snacks · flavoured pickles
Website ↗@goodgirlsnacks ↗

Positioning: against "an aisle that hasn't been revolutionised in years", the sleepy legacy pickle shelf, "a sea of glass jars with chartreuse and blue lids." The product is framed as a mindset, not a condiment: "hot girl energy," "we make pickles sexy," pickles as "Gen Z's avocados." The single most Uncrumpled-like brand here.

Palette & identity
#10141D
#CE2D1F
#FFCD53
#FF79AB
#806AE5
#359C96

Bold flavour-coded neon on near-black navy, so each SKU pops against the legacy chartreuse-lid crowd. Playful editorial-meets-doodle look: hand-drawn scribbles, star (★) motifs, quirky display type, and a mascot "Pik", an anthropomorphic pickle drawn deliberately not too pickle-like. The 🥒 emoji is the de facto logo. (Real hex, from live site CSS.)

Signature formats (from their IG)
  • Recipe reel — a genuinely useful brine-forward demo. Pickle Pasta Salad (99.5K views, 4,444 likes), top performer by far.
  • "Maturing is…" relatable-girl POV — "maturing is putting a whole jar in your beach sandwiches" (17K views).
  • Collab drop — the HOP WTR "pickle lemonade" launch, hype-caps.
  • Tease post — near-zero copy, farms comments (caption just "👀" = 75 comments).
  • Where-to-buy retail flex — stockist roundups (Whole Foods, Erewhon, HEB) that double as distribution proof.
The six lenses
1 · Villain

The stagnant legacy pickle aisle, a wall of identical chartreuse-and-blue-lidded jars with zero personality. Never a named competitor, they make the whole category look tired by contrast and plant their neon jars inside Whole Foods and Erewhon as the obvious upgrade.

2 · Emotion + community

Belonging to a hot-girl in-group, pickles as identity marker. Copy is peer-to-peer and lowercase ("we're summeringgggg," "wake up babe") so it reads like a friend in the group chat. Comment-bait does the work (the "👀" post = 3 likes but 75 comments). A mission layer too: inspiring young women to start companies.

3 · Guilt → pride

Guilt-free snacking flipped into a flex. They never use diet-guilt language; "hot girls eat pickles" makes the act aspirational, and the trademarked "yassifying your lunch™" turns a meal into something you post. Underdog pride too: two 23-year-olds, Erewhon's #1 pickle, on $0 ads.

4 · Branding

Owns "Hot Girl," "Good Girl," "yassifying your lunch™," "summering." All-caps hype for launches, lowercase-intimate for lifestyle, 🥒 as the logo. Flavour-coded neon-on-navy + doodles + the "Pik" mascot.

5 · Build in public

Strong and deliberate. Founders Leah & Yasaman (best friends who quit corporate at 23) run the podcast circuit and their own TikTok breaking down the branding, and broadcast milestones as content (10K jars, 50+ retailers, a pre-seed). It's a two-best-friends story you watch scale.

6 · Entertainment

Highest performers are entertaining first: the Pickle Pasta Salad reel (99.5K/4,444) is save-and-share utility, the "maturing is…" reel is a send-to-a-friend meme, and timely collabs ("pickle lemonade summer") ride a moment. The formula, recipe utility + relatable humour + timely collabs = organic shares on $0.

Deep dive 04
Poppi
Prebiotic soda
Website ↗@drinkpoppi ↗

Positioning: "the future of soda" and the redemption of a guilty pleasure, a full-flavoured soda you don't have to feel bad about (prebiotics, ≤5g sugar). The enemy is old-school sugary soda (~40g). Tagline lineage: "soda's back" → "sip like a hero" → "be gut happy."

Palette & identity
#EC008C
#FFF200
#00AEEF
#334FB4
#030303

Loud, candy-bright, retro-pop. Type is ITC Avant Garde Gothic, a rounded 1970s geometric sans that reads playful and nostalgic (not clinical wellness). Cans are hyper-saturated with heavy "cold sweat" condensation on colour-blocked backgrounds. Poppi Pink #EC008C is so ownable the vending machines needed no logo to read as Poppi. (Exact hex, from site CSS.)

Signature formats (from their IG)
  • "Which flavour should we make?" polls — the account's biggest comment engine. 32,943 likes / 3,265 comments.
  • Like-follow-tag giveaways — Lemonade giveaway pulled 8,276 comments.
  • Aspirational fridge / lifestyle b-roll — "the only way we want our fridge to look all summer" (278K views).
  • New-flavour / limited-edition drops — Spider-Man cans, Target dorm collection.
  • Culture tie-in "besties" content — Love Island villa watch-party packs + "poppi bingo."
The six lenses
1 · Villain

Traditional sugary soda, and the false choice of "tastes good vs good for you." Never preachy, "soda's back" keeps the joy minus the guilt. Nostalgic flavour names (Root Beer, Cherry Cola) mimic the enemy, then undercut it with the sugar/prebiotic stats.

2 · Emotion + community

Their strongest lens by the data, co-creation and belonging. Followers are "besties" with real agency: the flavour vote pulled 3,265 comments and a giveaway 8,276, both dwarfing the pretty product videos. Low-lift, high-reward interaction makes them feel like insiders shaping the brand.

3 · Guilt → pride

Soda as guilty pleasure → a badge of a smart, health-aware choice you post proudly. "Be gut happy," "sip like a hero." A Poppi-stocked fridge is a flex (278K views). Guilt is removed by design (the low-sugar stat); the aesthetic and community carry the pride.

4 · Branding

Owns "besties," "be gut happy," "soda's back," "sip like a hero," and 🩷🫧. Poppi Pink is the single strongest asset. Avant Garde retro-geometric type, condensation-heavy cans on saturated colour blocks.

5 · Build in public

Two layers: flavour polls build the roadmap in public then ship it (the Lemonade launch = a community-requested flavour delivered back), and founder Allison Ellsworth's origin is told openly (Shark Tank nine months pregnant; a TikTok that did $100K in 24 hours). She even addressed the vending backlash herself.

6 · Entertainment

A culture brand: it inserts into what fans already watch and hands them a game (Love Island "poppi bingo," watch-party packs), plus LE collabs (Spider-Man, Target) and Super Bowl stars (Charli xcx).

Cautionary (the vending-machine misfire, Feb 2025): Poppi shipped hot-pink vending machines to ~32 influencers. It backfired hard, read as tone-deaf (expensive free machines to already-wealthy creators while normal fans pay per can), and rival Olipop piled on. An influencer-gifting flex that reads as exclusionary can invert a "for-the-besties" community overnight. Also: Poppi's gut-health claim triggered an $8.9M class-action settlement, a reminder that any guilt-to-pride claim must be substantiation-safe.
Deep dive 05
Dr. Squatch
Men's natural soap & grooming
Website ↗@drsquatch ↗

Positioning: natural bar soap as the manly, honest alternative to mass-market "body wash," reframed as synthetic detergent, the stuff you'd use on a dish. "You're not a dish. You're a man." Budget caveat: it runs on celebrities (Megan Fox, Sydney Sweeney), licensed IP (SEGA, Disney) and Super Bowl money, borrow the mechanics, not the spend.

Palette & identity
#31572C*
#4A6B3A*
#E9E1CE*
#2B2622*
#D97D2B*

Rugged-outdoor-meets-vintage-apothecary. A pipe-smoking Sasquatch mascot, a "Squatch script" wordmark styled like a doctor's signature, kraft/cream boxes used as a canvas for on-pack jokes. Consciously avoids the lavender/pastel "farmer's market" look. (*approx, from packaging.)

Signature formats (from their IG)
  • Interactive cleaning content — "What should we clean next? Comment below 👀" (88.7K views).
  • Licensed collab drops — SEGA Sonic and Disney/Pixar Toy Story 5, written in a mock spec-sheet voice ("SCENT… GRIT - Zero").
  • Cinematic celebrity "curriculum" ads — the F.O.X. / Professor Fox series (6.89M views, 843K likes).
  • Deadpan one-liner statics — "THE ANSWER IS NO."
  • Product easter-egg reveals — "Bet you didn't know about this secret soap holder 😳" (27.3K).
The six lenses
1 · Villainstrongest

Named and crystal-clear: body wash = synthetic detergent, the same chemistry as dish soap, so the man using it is "washing himself like a plate." A category-creation move, they attack a format, not a rival, so every competitor is guilty at once. Second front: aluminium in deodorant.

2 · Emotion + community

Belonging + belly-laugh. The tribe is named, "Squatch Nation," addressed directly and farmed with comment-bait. They lean INTO fan chaos, e.g. the disclaimer post "Don't eat my products… pls stop asking" (107 comments) turns a running in-joke into content. 🌲 is the tribal signature.

3 · Guilt → pridecore engine

Shame setup (you've washed with dish detergent, your stick doesn't work, embarrassing) → pride payoff (a man who's handled it). "Say goodbye to stinky pits and hello to Odor Excellence" → "Let Your Stick Do The Talking." Crucially the shame is aimed at the old product, never cruelly at the man.

4 · Branding

Owns "You're not a dish, you're a man," "Squatch Nation," "Odor Excellence / F.O.X.," and mock spec-sheet vocabulary ("SCENT / GRIT"). Forest-green + cream + kraft, pipe-smoking Sasquatch, doctor's-signature wordmark, 🌲 sign-off, unmistakable in one line with no logo.

5 · Build in publichistorical only

Not a current-feed behaviour. The equity lives in the origin myth (Haldrup mixing soap in his garage for his psoriasis, his sister drawing the first Sasquatch), but the feed is polished campaign output, not an open kitchen.

6 · Entertainmentstrongest

Entertainment IS distribution. The Megan Fox "Professor Fox" academy hit 6.89M views / 843K likes, two orders of magnitude above baseline, proof that celebrity + a fictional universe is their sharpest lever. Note: F.O.X. is celebrity-dependent, not a low-cost mascot format.

Deep dive 06 · the scrappy one
Surreal
High-protein, zero-sugar cereal (UK)
Website ↗@eat_surreal ↗

Positioning: "the cereal you loved as a kid, made nutritionally relevant to the adult you've become" (zero sugar, high protein). The enemy is two-headed: legacy sugary kids' cereal and its mascot machine ("No tigers were hurt"), plus joyless cardboard health cereal. The most important brand here for you, because it wins big on a scrappy, no-budget playbook.

Palette & identity
#0A0A0A*
#FFFFFF
#F5F1E6*
#F5D000*
#1FB5AB*

A "Bowlface" mascot (a smiling face with a cereal bowl for a mouth) and a bold, grown-up sans. Two registers at once: (1) clean, premium black-and-white packaging (white logo = a nod to milk); (2) a deliberately ANTI-design OOH world (Comic Sans, clipart, plain text) engineered to look cheap so it reads as honest and gets photographed. (*approx.)

Signature formats (from their IG)
  • "Just a reminder we make cereal" — a deadpan nudge that undercuts advertising itself ("there's a dog at the end as a treat", 1,933 likes / 267 comments).
  • Deliberate typo gag — "NEW Banola Granana… depending on whether you value good spelling. We never have and never will."
  • Fake-footballer namesake ads — "budgets don't stretch to real footballers yet", so they use namesakes.
  • Cheeky-innuendo one-liner over a simple product clip.
  • Founder confessional — "Did I spend company money? Yes. My own money? No. Therefore it's free." (505 likes, 6,400 views).
The six lenses
1 · Villain

A clearly named enemy: the sugary kids'-cereal establishment AND the celebrity-endorsement industry itself. They can't afford real stars, so they hire namesakes and say so, and the fake-celebrity billboards blew past Kellogg's before the lawyers stepped in. The villain is the category's whole way of behaving, which lets a tiny brand punch up.

2 · Emotion + community

In-joke belonging, fans are rewarded for being smart enough to get the bit ("WTF IS SURREAL?" = 267 comments of banter). Nostalgia is the deeper engine. Community is literal too: they sold billboard space to customers and put fans' names in ads. All achieved through tone, not production spend.

3 · Guilt → pride

Cereal usually triggers diet guilt; Surreal flips it to pride (the childhood cereal you crave, but zero sugar / high protein) and refuses to preach, making the flip funny. The anti-January "Can't be bothered this January? Same." removes the shame that wellness marketing normally weaponises.

4 · Branding

Owns a deadpan, anti-advertising, friend-texting-you voice: deliberate misspelling as a value, stretched-letter copy, the running "we can't afford X" budget gag. It's so distinctive you could cover the visual and still name the brand from the tone. Premium B&W "Bowlface" packaging vs throwaway Comic-Sans OOH.

5 · Build in public

Strongly literal. Budget confessions ("didn't stretch to actual footballers"), spend-shaming ("might be the stupidest thing we've spent money on"), and the "Marketing for Marketers" billboard (500+ comments). The scrappiness IS the content, the small budget is a feature they advertise.

6 · Entertainment

Dominant, and the key lesson for a small brand: big video views on a tiny follower base, driven by jokes not spend. Their OOH is built to be photographed and reshared (fake celebs, Comic Sans, a "vandalised" billboard), turning paid placements into free earned reach. Their reach is a tone-and-idea budget, not a media budget.

Bringing it home

Now, Uncrumpled.

Every lesson above, mapped deep onto the anti-ironing spray, with content ideas per lens. You already own lenses 1 and 2. The two you've barely touched, and where the real feeling and community come from, are 3 (guilt → pride) and 5 (build in public).

Lens 1

Villain

The lesson: Dr. Squatch attacks a format ("body wash = detergent"), not a rival. Surreal attacks the whole category's behaviour. Liquid Death attacks corporate seriousness itself. Naming a category-wide enemy implicates every competitor at once and lets a small brand punch up.
Your villain isn't just the iron, it's the whole "supposed-to" life: the tedious chores, the pressure to be perfect, saying yes to things you don't want to do but do anyway. Ironing is the sharpest symbol of it. Attack the behaviour (the ritual, the perfect-house performance), not a competitor spray, and put the villain on screen so choosing Uncrumpled feels like defecting. Dr. Squatch's line "you're not a dish, you're a man" is the exact energy: name the absurdity out loud.
Content: the cold, sterile ironing frame (from the moodboard) cut hard against warm real life · "the supposed-to list" (Nan's rules) · a piece of copy that names the enemy, e.g. "ironing is a scam invented to steal your Sunday."
Lens 2

Emotion + community

The lesson: Poppi's flavour-vote pulled 3,265 comments (vs pretty product shots) by giving besties real agency. Hot Girl Pickles hands fans a flag ("hot girls eat pickles") they say about themselves. Liquid Death monetised belonging into a 225K-member club. Nobody sells the product.
Sell the emotion (the exhale, the weight off your shoulders, permission to not be perfect) and the community (Type C people who'd rather live than tidy), not the spray. Give the tribe a line they claim as their own the way "hot girls eat pickles" is claimed. And farm community with low-lift interaction (polls, tags, reposts), not demos, that's what actually pulls the comments.
Content: a chantable identity line fans repeat · "tag the friend who still irons" · reposting the tribe's real messy-living clips · the visible "ahh" relief moment.
Lens 3

Guilt → pride · untapped

The lesson: Dr. Squatch aims the shame at the old product, never at the man, then hands back status. Poppi removes guilt by design and lets the aesthetic carry pride. Graza dissolves hoarding guilt into daily-use pride. Hot Girl Pickles turns snacking into "yassifying your lunch." None of them preach.
Skipping the iron isn't lazy, it's smart and earned. Flip the guilt out loud: you don't need the 30-minute morning, the perfect house, the pressed-shirt performance. Aim the shame squarely at the old ritual ("ironing is the scam"), never at the person, exactly like Squatch. Make not-ironing a status move ("I haven't touched an iron in a year") and let the warm, put-together life carry the pride.
Content: "you don't need a 30-minute morning to look good" · "nobody remembers the ironing" · the ironing-math (the time you bought back) · permission-slip framing.
Lens 4

Branding: language + look

The lesson: Graza owns Sizzle/Drizzle (the name is the instruction). Liquid Death owns "Murder your thirst" + a gothic can. Poppi owns pink #EC008C so hard a machine needs no logo. Each owns verbs AND a look so distinctive you'd ID it with the logo covered.
You already own the language: "less ironing, more living," "spray it and go," "sorry Nan." Push them until they're yours alone, and keep coining ownable verbs. The look matters just as much, commit to the warm cream / sage / terracotta + Fraunces world from the moodboard so a post is unmistakably Uncrumpled with the logo covered, the exact opposite of the cold laundry aisle. Worth considering a signature emoji or mascot, the way 🥒, 🌲 and the Sasquatch anchor those brands.
Content: a consistent on-screen-text + end-card line on every video · a locked signature colour + type lockup · captions that always sound like Ann, never a brand.
Lens 5

Build in public · untapped

The lesson: Hot Girl Pickles' two-best-friends serial + $0-ad milestones. Poppi's Allison origin + flavour co-creation. Surreal's budget confessions ("did I spend company money? yes"). People root for a person and a journey, not a logo, and Surreal proves the scrappiness itself is the content.
Ann is the biggest untapped asset. Run the founder journey as a serial, the wins, the losses, the scrappy hacks, "trying to make ironing obsolete," building it in the open. You don't need polish, you need honesty, Surreal gets huge reach precisely by advertising how little it spends. Take the audience along and they become insiders cheering you on. (It's the same lever from the pep-talk that started this: build the thing all the way, out loud, and bring people with you.)
Content: the founder-grenade ("I spent $X on a video, here's what happened") · a running build-in-public diary · milestone posts (first 1,000 bottles, first retailer) · Surreal-style budget-confession humour.
Lens 6

Entertainment + shareable

The lesson: Liquid Death's "Size matters" (13.9M) vs a coupon post (293 likes), and Graza's "touch some glass" (904K) beating every recipe. Surreal wins on a tone budget, not a media budget. Every post is a bit, not an ad.
This is where lenses 1–5 combine. Once ironing is the villain, the emotion is clear, the guilt is flipped, the language and look are owned and Ann is in it, every post becomes a bit worth sharing. You don't need Dr. Squatch's budget (celebrities, Super Bowl), you need Surreal's and Hot Girl Pickles' cheek, scrappy, funny, native. Make the entertainment the vehicle and smuggle the sell inside it.
Content: the two holidays · less-of-this-more-of-this · Cool Grandma · the reset · a deadpan "we can't be bothered ironing either" à la Surreal.
The one guardrail, from Poppi: keep the fun warm and inclusive, never smug, their vending-machine flex to rich creators inverted their "for-the-besties" equity overnight. And any guilt-to-pride claim must stay substantiation-safe: never oversell what the spray actually does.
