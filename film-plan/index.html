Concept 1 · Film plan · Video 4

Top 3 things to pack

ListicleProduct nestedTravel
Format
Listicle to camera or hands only, filmed against the open case. 15 to 20 sec.
The concept
Three things you always pack. Uncrumpled sits at position two, never at the end and never as the reveal. The other two items are real things you actually pack, so the list is not a set up for the product.
Why this one
The nested-list rule exists because a list that ends on the product reads as an ad and a list that hides it mid-way reads as a recommendation. Travel is the number one theme across all 110 reviews, so this is the cheapest way into the strongest angle.
How to shoot it

Open case, already packed, on the bed or a bench.

Item one out of the case, held up, named. Something unglamorous and real.

Item two, the travel bottle. Say what it does in one line and move on. Do not linger.

Item three. Something that gets a reaction on its own.

Zip the case. End there. No pitch.

Props
Suitcase, packed. Vacuum sealer, shower cap, probiotics, bags for dirty clothes. Travel bottle.
Do not let the bottle be the punchline. If the edit lands on it, the format is broken. No timing claims on the one line about what it does.

Concept 5 · New this session

The timed demo

ProofOne unbroken takeCounter on screen
Format
Locked-off single take, no cuts, running counter burned in over the top. 20 to 30 sec.
The concept
Camera does not move and the take does not cut. Counter starts at zero on screen. Crumpled shirt, spray, smooth, hold it up. Counter is still running when the shot ends. Whatever the real duration turns out to be is what it says.
Why this one
Every wrinkle-spray demo online is cut, so the assumption is that all of them are cut for a reason. A locked camera plus a clock that never stops is the only version of the demo that cannot have been helped in the edit. It answers the doubt without arguing with it. This is the visual proof that Cluster 5 makes verbally.
How to shoot it

Tripod, locked off. Do not touch it once the take starts.

Even light edge to edge across the garment. Uneven light reads as a trick even when nothing was faked.

Start rolling, then start the counter. Zero on screen before the first spray.

Spray, smooth, hold it up. One continuous move.

Let the counter keep running past the reveal. Do not freeze it, do not cut on a round number.

Props
Cotton or linen shirt, crumpled evenly. Flat surface or a rail with even light. Tripod. Bottle. Counter goes on in the edit, not on the day.
THIS ONE HAS A RULE COLLISION, READ IT. Timing claims are hard-banned in Uncrumpled copy: "in seconds" was stripped from every ad on 13 Jul, and both Video 6 and the four-minute concept say show the effort, never put a number on it. A visible counter is footage of what happened rather than a claim, which is why it survives at all. To keep it that way: no number in the headline, no number in the primary text, no number as on-screen text, and no target time. The counter is the only place a number appears anywhere in the ad. Do not cut a version that races an iron, because a comparison with a clock on it is a timing claim no matter how it is filmed.
Concept 6 · New this session

10,000 orders

To cameraFounderSocial proof in one object
Format
To camera, holding the thing up. Short. 20 to 30 sec. Shoot it a little unpolished.
The concept
You hold up the trophy and say what it is for. Not a flex and not a thank-you video. It is one object that tells a stranger other people already bought this, which is the job a review normally does.
Why this one
Cold audience has no reason to believe you yet, and a number spoken to camera by the founder while holding the physical marker for it does more than the same number typed on a static. It is also the only proof asset in the set that needs no garment, no demo and no second location.
How to shoot it

Hold it up first, before you explain it. The object earns the three seconds.

Say what it is and what it is for, in one line.

One line on where it started. Spray bottle, a hatred of ironing, no idea anyone else would care.

One line on what it actually means, which is people, not a number.

Stop. No CTA. Let Tail C carry it, or nothing at all.

Props
The trophy. Bottle optional in frame. Clean backdrop.
TWO THINGS TO CONFIRM BEFORE THIS ROLLS. First, the order count still contradicts itself in our own scripts: 10,000 in seven months in one place, 13,000 in eight months in another. It has been flagged twice and never settled. You say the number out loud in this one, so it cannot be fixed in the edit. Pick one and stick to it across the whole shoot. Second, confirm what the trophy actually is and whether it names a figure on it, because if the object says one number and you say another on camera the ad is unusable.
Concept 7 · Ad concepts · AD3

Six steps, one shirt

Counted listOne shot per stepSound off
Built for
Jas the office professional, Deb, Billy. One setup, three versions, only the person counting changes.
Format
Counted list, one shot per step, fast cut. 12 sec.
The concept
Board out, unfold, plug in, wait, press, wait, fold, put away. Number each step on screen as it happens. Then one shot at the end: spray, smooth. No number on that one.
Why this one
Puts the whole villain on screen without ever naming the customer. The comedy is the count, not a punchline, and the missing number on the last shot does the arguing. Reads with the sound off, which is where most of the impressions are.
How to shoot it

Board comes out of wherever it lives. Number 1.

Unfold it, set the legs. Number 2.

Find the cord, plug it in. Number 3.

Wait for the light. Hold this shot longer than is comfortable. Number 4.

Press. Number 5.

Fold it, put it away, walk it back to where it lives. Number 6.

Hard cut. Spray, smooth. No number appears.

Same shoot, different person
  • Jas. Weeknight, one office shirt, all six steps.
  • Deb. She knows all six by heart and does them in order without looking. That is the joke.
  • Billy. Hotel version, board out at 6am in a room he is leaving in an hour.

What changes is who is counting and what room it is in. The six moves never change.

Props
Ironing board, iron, one office shirt. Bottle for the last shot.
The waiting shots are the ad. If the edit trims them to keep it snappy the argument disappears. No timing claim on screen or in copy, the count does that job instead.
Concept 8 · Swipe file 14 Aug · A3 Loop

What it does not do

To cameraOne takeNo demo
Built for
All five. It is the only ad in the set that speaks to everyone by narrowing what it promises.
Format
Ann to camera, one take, flat delivery. 15 to 20 sec. There is deliberately no demo in this one.
The concept
Open by giving away the thing that would have been the one star review. The polyester limit is the first line of the ad, not a footnote on someone else’s.
Why this one
Loop run this as their answer to the single most asked question, and it doubles as their best converting angle. The concession is what makes everything after it read as true, and it filters the buyer so the people who do order already know exactly what they bought. We have a real limit sitting in this account as a warning tag on other ads. This turns it into the opening line of its own.
How to shoot it

To camera, no music. “The question I get asked more than any other is whether it works on everything.”

Beat. “No. It does not work on polyester.” Say it flat. Do not soften it and do not smile through it.

“It works on cotton. Linen. Viscose. Rayon. Blends of those.” Hold each fabric up as it is named, or do not, but keep the list plain.

“If your work shirt is polyester, this is not for you, and I would rather tell you now than have you find out.”

Close: “Everything else, it gets the creases out and gets you out the door.” Stop there.

Same shoot, different person
  • Second version, shorter: cut everything except the no and the fabric list. 8 sec.
  • Creator led version, matching the Loop ad Ann saved. Paid UGC only and it must stay product led.
  • Expectation setter variant: “It will not make a shirt look dry cleaned. It makes it look like you are not the sort of person who irons.”

What changes is length and who says it. The concession is always the first thing out of anyone’s mouth.

Props
Bottle. One cotton shirt, one linen shirt. No board, no iron, nothing to demonstrate with.
Reference, the ad this came from
Loop Earplugs

The softer creator led version. She frames it around a real situation, hotels and sleepovers, then compares honestly against the cheap disposables that fall out rather than claiming everything. The harder version of the concession is the other Loop ad in the swipe file, where they answer the most asked question with "honestly, yes you will".

Open in the Meta Ad Library
Flat delivery, no apology, no joke to soften it, no music sting. The moment it sounds defensive the mechanic breaks. DO NOT add a demo to the end, the absence of proof is what makes it read as honest. Do not use the phrase dry cleaning as a comparison anywhere except the scripted expectation setter line, which exists specifically to kill that comparison.
Concept 9 · Swipe file 14 Aug · E3 TUSHY

This one is for you

One setupFive openersVolume engine
Built for
One per persona. Billy, Jas twice, Mia, Sienna.
Format
One body, filmed once. Five different first lines recorded separately and cut onto the front. 12 to 15 sec each.
The concept
Write the whole ad for one narrow group and lose everybody else in the first line, on purpose. TUSHY run three of these at once, one for people with IBS, one for women on GLP-1s, one for flare days, and nobody else is addressed at any point in any of them.
Why this one
Not a persona in the targeting, a persona in the script. They are willing to lose 95% of the audience in the first three seconds, which is exactly why the 5% stop. It is also the correct execution of the callout direction locked back in July and never properly shot, and it is the cheapest variant we have: one clip, one line changed on the front, five ads out.
How to shoot it

Film the BODY once, and never let it mention who it is for. “This is what I use instead. Spray it, smooth it with your hand, put it on. No board, no cord, nothing to plug in.”

Then record the five openers separately, straight to camera, one take each.

Opener 1, Billy: “If you are a flight attendant, this one is for you.”

Opener 2, Jas: “If you get changed at work, this is for you.”

Opener 3, Jas: “If you are back at your desk by two and the shirt has already gone, this is for you.”

Opener 4, Mia: “If you are the one who irons somebody else’s work shirts, this is for you.”

Opener 5, Sienna: “If you have booked the flights and you are already thinking about what you can pack, this is for you.”

Same shoot, different person
  • Hands only version of the body, so the openers can be voiceover and the whole thing needs no reshoot to add a sixth persona later.

Only the first line changes. Record the openers back to back in one sitting, they are ten seconds each.

Props
One shirt, bottle. Nothing else.

TUSHY

Concept 10 · Swipe file 14 Aug · D2 Surreal

I am not going to explain it

Deadpan10 secNo bullets
Built for
All five. It is a voice piece rather than a persona piece.
Format
Ann to camera, deadpan, no music, no on screen bullets. 10 to 12 sec.
The concept
Refuse to explain the product and say so out loud. Surreal run ad copy that reads “we’d explain why but honestly we just want you to try it”, and it is the least ad shaped thing in their whole library.
Why this one
Refusing to sell is a sell. It signals the product is obvious enough not to need a pitch and it reads as a person typing rather than a brand writing copy. It also sidesteps the exact thing that makes our current ads look like ads, which is the block of five ticks. This is the cheapest test in the file and it sits naturally in the un- and anti-perfection voice.
How to shoot it

“I could tell you about hydrogen bonds.”

“I could tell you it took sixteen goes to get this right.”

Beat. “You do not care.”

“It is a spray. You spray it. The creases drop out.”

End card: Spray. Smooth. Done. Nothing after it.

Same shoot, different person
  • Copy only version, no shoot at all: put “I could write you a whole thing about hydrogen bonds. Or you could just try it.” as the body text on an existing hero video.
  • End card variant: “There is a long explanation. Nobody wants it.” then the locked sign off.

The copy only version needs no shoot and is the actual test. The filmed version is the upside.

Props
Bottle. Nothing else. Plain backdrop.
Reference, the ad this came from
Surreal

Watch the ad copy rather than the video. It reads "53% off cereal with 18g protein and less than 1g sugar. We would explain why but honestly we just want you to try it." Same brand also runs "Not a protein bar pretending to be breakfast. Not a supplement. Cereal."

Open in the Meta Ad Library
Use anti-explanation on the OFFER and the PRODUCT, never on the mechanism or the polyester limit, because those two genuinely do need explaining. No timing claim on the creases drop out line. Run this as a straight A/B against the locked five tick bullet block on the same video, otherwise you learn nothing.

Clusters, body plus hooks

Longest job on the day, so it goes last. Six persona-led clusters. Each one is a single body plus its own set of three to five second openers, so one body gives you up to six ads. Hooks never move between clusters and a body never restates its own hook.
Cluster 1 · body

Gets changed at work

Persona

Alex, the office professional. Corporate or client-facing, gets dressed somewhere that is not home.

Angle

The shirt was fine when you packed it. It is not fine when it comes out, there is no iron wherever you are, and now you are late as well.

What has to match between the hooks and this body
The demo has to be on a shirt pulled straight out of a bag with hard fold lines across it. Not a generically scrunched shirt. Every hook promises the bag moment, so the body has to show the bag moment.
Body script

You go to get changed. You pull your shirt out of your bag and it has that fold line straight across the middle, or it is scrunched to no end.

So now you either wear it like that, or you go looking for an iron that is not going to be there.

And now you are fifteen minutes late on top of it.

(pull the shirt from the bag, spray, smooth, hold it up)

This lives in my bag. Non-toxic, and it washes out in the next wash.

Then
Tail A, shop the link below.
Props
Work bag or tote. Two shirts folded into it hard the night before so the creases are real. Bottle.
Body line two says "an iron that is not going to be there" rather than naming an office, so it still fits the dinner hook and the cycling hook, not only the office one.

Cluster 2 · body

Get the twenty minutes back

Persona

Mia, the working mum, and anyone who is up earlier than they need to be. Core persona and the widest audience in the account.

Angle

Ironing time is sleep you are giving away. Stop doing it and you get the time back, so the sell is the extra twenty minutes, not the stress.

What has to match between the hooks and this body
The morning in this body has to look calm, not frantic. Every hook promises more sleep and a later start, so a chaotic rushing body would contradict the offer. Later alarm, unhurried, out the door on time.

I used to be up before everyone else just to stand at an ironing board.

Now I get up when I actually need to get up, and this is the entire reason why.

(spray, smooth, put it on, unhurried)

Dressed, out, and nobody knows the difference.

Non-toxic, and it washes out next wash.

Then
Tail A, shop the link below.
