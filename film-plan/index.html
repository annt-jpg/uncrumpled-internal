How this shoot works

Film the hooks. Reuse the bodies.

The body of an ad is the part that never really changes: the demo, the reasons, the story, the close. There is no point filming it nine times. Film each body once, properly, then film a large bank of three to five second openers that all cut into it.

Hook, 3 to 5 sec+Body+Tail=one ad
32 hooks and 5 bodies is not 37 videos, it is every workable pairing. Even filming only the pairings marked on each hook, one studio day covers the 20 to 30 a week the plan needs for over a month, and the variable being tested is clean: same body, same close, only the first three seconds move.

The launch videos already work this way, which is why they are grouped as one item each rather than three. USA, UK and NZ each share a single body with three different openers on the front.

⚑ Rules that make the pieces actually fit together
  • One outfit, one background, one camera position for the entire hook block. Change any of them and every cut becomes visible. Do the hooks in one unbroken run.
  • Never name a colour or a garment in a hook. The body behind it might show a different shirt.
  • No claims in a hook. Every claim lives in a body, so it gets written once and checked once.
  • End each hook clean. No trailing word, no answering your own setup. The body answers it.
  • Start every body already mid-action so a hook can cut straight in with no dead beat.
  • Shoot each hook twice: once down the lens, once already moving. The second version saves cuts that feel static.
  • Say the hook number out loud before each take and record 30 seconds of room tone before you start. Both save hours in the edit.
  • No "in seconds" or any timing claim. No em dashes on screen. No "made in Australia", say Australian brand.

Bodies to film

Body 1

The demo

Universal middleNo talkingShoot 4 garments
The middle that sits behind almost every hook. Crumpled garment, spray, smooth, hold it up. Start already mid-action so any hook can cut straight in without a beat of dead air.
How to shoot it

Open on hands already on the garment. Do not start on a still shirt, do not start on your face.

Spray, smooth, hold up. One continuous move.

Shoot the whole thing four times with four garments: white shirt, denim shirt, checkered shirt, linen pants.

Silent. No line, no reaction. Anything you say limits which hooks it fits behind.

Props
Four garments, all crumpled the same way. Full bottle. Hanging rail or flat surface.

Body 4

The proof, half and half

One garmentSilentHighest trust
One shirt, spray one half on camera, then one unbroken pan across both halves. The studio version of the pants idea. Nobody can argue it was two different shirts because they never leave the frame.
How to shoot it

Hold the shirt up crumpled, both halves in shot, so it is obvious they start the same.

Spray the left half only, on camera.

Lay it flat. One continuous pan or hand-run across both halves. Do not cut.

Labels go on in the edit: "sprayed" and "not sprayed".

Props
One cotton or linen shirt. Bottle. Flat surface with even light across the whole garment.
Even light matters more than anything else here. A shadow on one half and the whole thing reads as a trick.
Body 5

The effort gap

ComparisonReal timeFunny by itself
The full iron routine against a spray. Board out, plug in, wait, press every inch. Let it drag on purpose. The joke is the length.
How to shoot it

Do the entire iron routine in real time. Do not speed it up, the tedium is the point.

Cut to the spray version, same garment type, same framing.

End on both garments held up together.

Props
Iron and board, two matching crumpled shirts, bottle.
No timing claims on either side. Show the effort, never put a number on it.
Tails

Three end cards

Film onceReused everywhere
Filmed once, cut onto the end of anything. Keeps the closes consistent and stops you improvising a different CTA every time.
How to shoot it

Tail A: bottle in hand, "Shop the link below to get yours."

Tail B: "Thirty day money back. If you do not love it, we will refund you."

Tail C: silent. Bottle placed down, hold two seconds. For anything that should not end on a pitch.

Props
Bottle, clean backdrop.

Hook bank · 32

Occupation and situation 8

The locked callout direction. Name the job or the situation, never a personality type. These are the highest-priority block: film all eight.

Confession openers 6

Low status, no selling. These carry the brand voice and they are the ones people finish.

Questions 5

Cheap, and they cut into anything. Ask it, do not answer it, let the body answer.

Objections 5

Each of these already exists as a reason someone did not buy. Say it before they think it.

Proof and authority 4

Every number in this block needs confirming before anything ships. Film them anyway, hold them until the numbers are locked.

No words, visual only 4

Text on screen, no voice. Cheapest to shoot and the only ones that survive with sound off.

Set piece

The skit, "give it here"

DemoTwo people (A + B)Objection handling
Reference
Lash-extensions talking-to-self ref video ↗
Setting
B is ready, waiting. A walks in with a wrinkled shirt, flustered.
Script

A: Sorry, give me another 20 minutes, I just need to iron this.

B: Iron? Give it here.

A: (skeptical) Wait... does that even actually work?

B: (spraying) Watch. (start demo)

A: Okay but I don't want chemicals sitting on my clothes all day.

B: (still smoothing) Non-toxic. Washes straight out next wash.

A: Is it gonna be all wet though?

B: (handing it back) Not really, and any small wet patches dry quickly.

A: (surprised, holding it up) ...Okay that's actually smooth.

B: (grabbing the bottle back) Get your own. This one's mine.

Props
Wrinkled shirt, Uncrumpled bottle.
Needs a second person on camera. Your no-characters rule is for your organic page, so confirm you are happy with someone in this one.
Set piece

Only good use for an iron

ComedyStudio friendlyNew
Straight-faced product review of an iron, for everything except clothes. Toastie press. Sealing a chip packet. Reheating last night's pizza. Score it out of ten as a kitchen appliance.
How to shoot it

Deliver it completely deadpan, like a genuine review.

Three uses maximum, the fourth stops being funny.

End by putting it away in a cupboard, not in a bin. It is not a funeral.

Props
Iron, bread and cheese, a chip packet, a plate. Baking paper so you do not wreck the iron.
This is not the iron funeral idea, which you killed. Nothing gets destroyed and nothing gets mourned.

Set piece

What if you never needed to iron?

To cameraFounder VSLLong form
The long-form version. Bold lines are delivered to camera, the rest can sit over demo cutaways. This one stays whole, but the opening line also works as hook 17 if you want it both ways.
Script

What if you never actually needed to iron?

One day I noticed something. When you want to get the wrinkles out of a shirt, everyone tells you the same thing: drag out the board, heat up the iron, wait around, press every inch. For one creased top. Or buy a steamer, fill the tank, wait for it to heat up, hold the steamer on it, but don't hold it too close or it might burn, and don't forget to empty the water afterwards or mould will grow.

Do I seriously need a whole appliance and half my morning just to look put together?

Now what if someone who genuinely hated ironing came along and made a spray purely to get wrinkles out. Something that wasn't just water and fragrance in a bottle. No heat, no board, obsessed with actually working and being non-toxic enough to use every single morning. Wouldn't that be good?

I thought so. So that's what I did.

Uncrumpled started from my hatred of ironing, so I decided to do something about it. I tried everything out there, all useless or as much work as just ironing. Then my dad got diagnosed with cancer, and I reformulated the whole thing to be non-toxic and quick-drying.

Sixteen samples later I had it, and now I'm shipping it all over Australia with a ton of reviews from people who never want to touch an iron again.

So if you like the sound of getting the wrinkles out with just a spray and not much else, check it out. Plus, we're so confident in it that if you don't love it, we'll give you your money back.

Props
You to camera plus a demo cutaway to drop over the "made a spray" beat. Body 1 covers the cutaway.
