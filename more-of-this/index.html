Less ironing. More living.

The first and only mention of ironing in the whole film. Five moments, then the trade, and it lands retrospectively.

Beautiful but ordinary, and this is the one that can go wrong. A backyard in summer and a coffee on the floorboards, not an infinity pool. The brand is anti perfection, so the moment it looks like a holiday brochure it stops being her audience's life and becomes an ad for somewhere else. Nothing here needed a flight to film.
Same voice as the other films, and it stays flat. Especially the last card. Less ironing, more living is the whole point of the film, so it should be read like somebody stating a preference, not delivering a slogan.
No iron and no ironing board in any frame. Not folded in a corner, not in the background. The film argues by absence, and showing the thing she is not doing hands the villain a cameo it does not need.

The feel, same as the other films

Watch it. The words here are doing far less work than the look is. It needs to be beautiful and it needs to look expensive. That is the whole reference.

Reference · the texture

Heels carried in the lift, brass taps, earrings at the mirror. Expensive, and everybody in it looks beautiful. That is the bar. The good clothes are treated as a character rather than a prop, the light does most of the work, and it holds every shot well past the point a reel normally cuts.
