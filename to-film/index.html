Going through the ingredients, one at a time

To cameraFive parts, one sittingStatics fall out of it
The only script with published research behind every line. Answers the question the comments ask most: what is actually in it.
The idea
Table, the bottle, good light, sat down. One ingredient at a time. She says what it does, then what the report says, and moves on. No music, no graphics, nothing clever. Five short parts filmed in one sitting so any one of them can run on its own.
Three hooks
  1. Everyone is worried about what they put on their skin, and rightfully so. So let me tell you what is in this one.
  2. Our ingredients, explained. All five of them.
  3. You would be surprised how many people tell me what is in my own product. I made it. So here is the actual list.
Script

Ann: I get comments about this all the time. What is in it. Is it full of chemicals.

Ann: I get it. So you should know I know exactly what is in this bottle, because I formulated it.

Ann: Sixteen versions before I was happy with it. There are five things in here. That is the whole list.

(picks up the bottle)

Ann: First off, the one that does the actual work. It relaxes what is holding the crease so the fabric can fall flat again. No heat, and nothing stiff or tacky left on the cloth.

Ann: Then water, because that is what carries it into the fibre. Then it goes.

Ann: One that spreads it out evenly, so you do not get wet patches.

Ann: A preservative, so it keeps. That one does nothing to your clothes, it protects what is in the bottle.

Ann: And one that takes the smell out of the fabric rather than covering it up.

Ann: Two of those five have been assessed as safe by the Cosmetic Ingredient Review panel, and found non-irritating in clinical testing. I will put the reports in the comments.

Ann: It does not work on polyester. Never has.

Each one also works alone, opening on the question
  • "What is the preservative in it, and is that bad?" Then just card 04.
  • "Is there silicone in this?" Then just card 02, and answer it straight.
  • "Why does it not work on polyester?" The honest limit as its own ad.
  • "What is actually in it?" All five, under thirty seconds, fast.
Props
The bottle, a plain table, daylight. Nothing else.
Say assessed as safe by the Cosmetic Ingredient Review panel. It is true, checkable, and stronger than any adjective.

S2

In the directors chair. The story of Unstain

InterviewDirectors chairTwo people, one off camera
The idea
Ann in a directors chair, someone off camera asking the questions out loud. Not a piece to camera. They ask, she answers, and the answer runs as long as it runs. Four questions in one sitting, and every answer cuts out as its own video.
Script

Person 1: Ann, can you tell us the story of Unstain and how it started.

Ann: We were at an all you can eat buffet for my sisters graduation.

Ann: My mother in law spilled her food down the front of her white shirt.

Ann: She ended up making my brother in law take his shirt off so she could wear it, because she could not sit there in hers.

Ann: And I thought, that is the second reason nobody wears the good stuff. Creased is the first one. I already fixed that one.

Person 1: And what was the process to getting here?

Ann: Long. And a lot of ruined clothes, most of them mine.

Ann: One sample turned a shirt green. That one did not make it.

Ann: And every time you make it stronger it gets harsher, so you go back and start again. That balance is the whole job. It has to actually work, and it has to be something I am happy putting near my kids.

Person 1: What makes it different to what is already out there?

Ann: Most of what exists is a laundry product. You get home, you treat it, you wash it.

Ann: This one is for the moment it happens, while you are still sat there. You wipe it and you carry on with your day.

Person 1: And what does it not do?

Ann: It will not save something you sat on three weeks ago, and it does not work on everything.

Ann: It is for what just happened to you.

(shows it, once, small)

Ann: And if you do not love it, thirty day money back.

Other questions worth asking in the same sitting
  • How many versions did you go through before this one?
  • What is the worst thing you have ever spilled on yourself?
  • What is the one thing you wish it did?
  • Why not just get it dry cleaned?
Props
Unstain, a directors chair, clean backdrop, someone off camera to ask.

HOW

How to use it. The tutorial

Hands and product onlyMethod has to be confirmed firstSame setup as the roll call
The idea
Flat surface, even light, one stained garment. No story and no scenario. She does it once, properly, at real pace, and names the things people get wrong on the way through.
Three hooks
  1. How to actually use it.
  2. Most people get this wrong. I did too, at first.
  3. Do not rub it. Here is why.
Script

(it lands on a white shirt)

Ann: First thing, get to it while it is still wet. That is most of it right there.

Ann: Do not rub it in. Press, lift, press again. You are picking it up, not spreading it around.

Ann: Work from the outside of the mark inwards, so you are not making it bigger than it was.

Ann: Then leave it alone. It looks worse while it is damp. It is not finished until it is dry.

Ann: And if it is a set stain from last week, this is not the thing for it. Nothing you carry in a bag is.

Each question is its own short version
  • Do you still need to wash it afterwards?
  • Does it work on colours, or only white?
  • What about a stain from yesterday?
  • Can you use it on something you are still wearing?
Props
White garments, a staining food, Unstain, flat surface, even light.
Everything in that script is generic textile advice, not our instructions. Blot rather than rub, outside in, judge it dry. It is written this way so it reads true, but confirm the lot against the manufacturer directions and the claims pack before filming, and do not name a fabric that is not on the pack. Do not claim it lifts an old set stain, the script says the opposite on purpose.
