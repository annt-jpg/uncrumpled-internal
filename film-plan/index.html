Video 5

What if you never needed to iron?

To cameraFounder VSLLong form
The long-form founder version. Bold lines to camera, the rest can sit over demo cutaways.
Script

What if you never actually needed to iron?

One day I noticed something. When you want to get the wrinkles out of a shirt, everyone tells you the same thing: drag out the board, heat up the iron, wait around, press every inch. For one creased top. Or buy a steamer, fill the tank, wait for it to heat up, hold the steamer on it, but don't hold it too close or it might burn, and don't forget to empty the water afterwards or mould will grow.

Do I seriously need a whole appliance and half my morning just to look put together?

Now what if someone who genuinely hated ironing came along and made a spray purely to get wrinkles out. Something that wasn't just water and fragrance in a bottle. No heat, no board, obsessed with actually working and being non-toxic enough to use every single morning. Wouldn't that be good?

I thought so. So that's what I did.

Uncrumpled started from my hatred of ironing, so I decided to do something about it. I tried everything out there, all useless or as much work as just ironing. Then my dad got diagnosed with cancer, and I reformulated the whole thing to be non-toxic and quick-drying.

Sixteen samples later I had it, and now I'm shipping it all over Australia with a ton of reviews from people who never want to touch an iron again.

So if you like the sound of getting the wrinkles out with just a spray and not much else, check it out. Plus, we're so confident in it that if you don't love it, we'll give you your money back.

Props
You to camera plus a demo cutaway to drop over the "made a spray" beat.

Video 8

Half and half, iron one leg, spray the other

Proof demoOne garmentNo talking needed
Same pair of pants, both legs equally crumpled. Iron the left leg properly on the board, spray and smooth the right. Then lay them flat so people compare two legs of one garment. Different to Video 6: that compares the effort, this compares the result on a single item so nobody can say it was two different pairs.
How to shoot it

Hold the pants up crumpled. Show both legs together so it is obvious they start the same.

Iron the left leg. Real time, no cut.

Spray and smooth the right leg. Same framing, same distance.

Lay them flat and run a hand down both legs in one continuous shot. Do not cut between legs, that is the whole point.

Put them on, walk out.

Props
Linen or cotton pants, not polyester. Iron and board. Bottle. Flat surface with even light on both legs.
One pair, one light, one take across both legs. The second you cut mid-comparison you lose the thing that makes it work. Labels go on in the edit: ironed and sprayed.

Cluster 5 · body

You think this does not work

Persona

Not a life stage, a state of mind. Someone who has bought a wrinkle spray that did nothing, or assumes all of them are water in a bottle.

Angle

Explain what a wrinkle actually is and why this works on it, then stop talking and prove it on one garment with one variable.

What has to match between the hooks and this body
Every hook here is a doubt or a challenge, so the body has to answer with mechanism first and proof second. A warm lifestyle hook on the front of this body wastes it.
Body script

A wrinkle is not dirt and it is not damage. It is the fibres in the fabric setting into a shape while they were pressed.

An iron breaks that shape with heat and pressure. This does it without either.

(hold up the bottle) Our CreaseDrop formula goes into the fibre and relaxes it, so it can drop back to how it is meant to sit.

Want proof, in real time? If you are sceptical, watch this.

(spray the left half only, lay it flat, one unbroken pan across both halves)

Same shirt, same minute, same light. That is the difference.

It will not do this on polyester, and I would rather tell you now than have you find out.

Then
Tail B, thirty day money back.
Props
One cotton or linen shirt, crumpled evenly across both halves. Flat surface with even light edge to edge. Bottle.
Two things before this ships. Run the wrinkle-and-fibre explanation past the manufacturer so the mechanism wording is accurate. CreaseDrop is approved public language, per your product-copy call. And even light across the whole garment matters more than anything else here: one shadow on one half and the proof reads as a trick.
