How Uncrumpled
becomes cool.

Five brands turned a boring product into something people want to belong to. Here's how we do the same, the lessons, the formula and the formats, aimed straight at Uncrumpled.

01 / The one idea

Stop selling a spray. Start selling an identity.

Not one of the brands we studied sells the product. Liquid Death doesn't sell water, it sells being an irreverent rebel. Hot Girl Pickles doesn't sell pickles, it sells being the hot girl. They sell who you become, and the product tags along.

The whole thing in one line

Uncrumpled gets cool the second it stops being "a spray that de-wrinkles clothes" and becomes the brand for people who reject the "supposed-to" life and choose to actually live.

Ironing is the sharpest, most relatable symbol of that, but the real enemy is bigger. Cool isn't a feature. It's a tribe you're proud to be in, an attitude, and a world people recognise. That's what we're building, not a better demo.

02 / What "cool" means for us

Six things that make a brand cool.

Every brand in the study had these. This is the checklist for Uncrumpled, and one of them (a recognisable world) is the one we don't have yet.

1A villain everyone secretly agrees with

Ours is "the supposed-to." You're supposed to iron, keep a perfect house, be nice to people who are mean to you, stay humble and small, put everyone else first, not take up space. Nobody truly loves those rules, so we're safe to make them the enemy.

2A tribe you're proud to join

Cool is belonging. We're building the people who live on their own terms, less ironing, more living, more authentic, less "supposed-to." You buy in to join them.

3An attitude, not politeness

Cool brands have a point of view. Ours is fun, cheeky, warm, a little rebellious ("sorry Nan"). We say what people are secretly thinking: forget the rules that don't serve you.

4A world you recognise instantly

Poppi = bright pink + bubbly type. Liquid Death = punk, metal, festivals. Ours is our green + a warm, retro, sun-faded filter on every video, plus our owned lines, so a post reads Uncrumpled with the logo covered. See section 04.

5Content that entertains first

Cool is worth watching, not an ad. We make people laugh or feel something, then smuggle the sell in. You don't need a big budget, you need cheek.

6A real human behind it

Cool is authentic. Ann, building it in the open, the wins, the losses, the scrappy hacks, gives people someone to root for.

03 / The formula

How the six lenses connect for us.

They're not separate boxes, they feed each other. This is the engine.

Villain + Pride → Community
The "supposed-to" (ironing, the perfect house, being small and humble) + pride ("I don't live like that anymore") → the tribe who live on their own terms.
Emotion + Branding → Community & Entertainment
The weight-off-your-shoulders feeling + our loud, fun, less-ironing-more-living world → belonging, and content people share.
Build in public → Trust
Ann's journey, shown honestly, adds trust under everything.
Entertainment → ties it together
Makes it all watchable. Not every post is buzzy (some are a plain demo), but entertainment is the thread.

You're supposed to iron. Supposed to keep the house perfect. Supposed to be nice to people who aren't nice to you, stay humble, keep small. Nah. If it's not authentic to you, forget it. Take up space, go on the adventure, live.

04 / Building our world

The world you'd recognise with the logo covered.

A recognisable world doesn't come from a logo, it comes from a repeatable sensory world you use so consistently that people know it's us in half a second. Three anchors, and they're now locked.

A signature filter / grade the big one

One warm, retro, slightly sun-faded look over every video, no exceptions. Same wash every time is the fastest way to become instantly recognisable. This alone starts to create "oh, that's Uncrumpled."

Owned on-screen type + lines

One font, one placement, and the same phrases every time: "less ironing, more living," "sorry Nan," "spray it and go." The words become the wallpaper of the brand.

Our colour: the green

We already own a green brand colour, so we lock it. The green + the warm retro filter are the two anchors of the Uncrumpled world. (No mascot, that's not us.)

05 / What we steal from each

One big move per brand, and how we do it.

Liquid Deaththe rebel
Treat content like entertainment and make the enemy an attitude. Comedy scales, coupons don't. How for us: show the life, not the spray. Liquid Death films pregnant mums partying with a water keg (finally, pregnancy isn't "boring, stay home"), the water's barely the point. Our funniest content might hardly show the bottle, the joke or the moment is the star, the spray is incidental.
Grazathe warm one
Villain through contrast, real people, branding bigger than the logo. They never mention old olive oil, you just feel the contrast. How for us: cast every kind of person, a grandson showing Nana "you don't have to iron anymore," kids, 30s-60s, any gender, bonding and living. Never name the iron. Let the warmth + desirability justify our premium price (people happily pay more for Graza too).
Hot Girl Picklesthe closest twin
Let the branding BE the emotion, an identity people wish they had. How for us: define our magnetic identity, the cool non-ironer: a bit of Liquid Death's rebel + Graza's everyone + a 30s-60s "fuck society's rules" energy. People should watch and think "I want to be that." Then the community forms around wanting in.
Poppithe aspirational one
Build a world, then infuse it into everyday routines. Poppi + Love Island = a Poppi while you watch. How for us: put our world inside the life people already live, "Uncrumpled while you watch your favourite show," "…before the school run," "…on the way out the door." Insert our world into their world. (Plus: culture tie-ins and creators, not giveaways.)
Dr. Squatchthe funny villain
Pose hard against the villain, then make funny content. How for us: comedy skits where the spray quietly saves the day, funny first, sell smuggled in. Ironing is our "average soap." You don't need their budget for the format, just the cheek.
06 / The moves

How we actually get cool.

Rough priority order. Now = start immediately. Later = once we've built an audience.

Sell the identity, not the spray now

Reframe every post around who you become (someone who lives on their own terms), not what the product does.

Name and play the villain now

Make the "supposed-to" (ironing + society's rules) the enemy, through contrast and humour, never mean, never a competitor.

Lock the look and the language now

Start the warm retro filter, on-screen type and owned lines today (section 04). Our green + the filter are the anchors. No mascot.

Build emotion with real people + culture now

Every kind of person bonding over living more (Graza), plus infusing our world into everyday routines (Poppi).

Flip guilt into pride now

Being a non-ironer is smart, not lazy, a flex. "I haven't touched an iron in a year, here's what I did instead."

Entertain first, on a cheek budget now

Comedy skits and relatable bits where the spray is incidental (Dr. Squatch's format, Surreal's cheek). Watchable first.

Put Ann in the open now

Build in public, "day X of building Uncrumpled." Trust, and a person to root for.

Build the tribe and give it a flag later

Once there's an audience, make "less ironing, more living" something people say about themselves, and give the non-ironers a name and a voice.

Community: repost real people + moments later

When customer content starts flowing, feature real people living the life. Not yet, we don't have the community, but this is the engine we grow into.

07 / What cool is NOT for us

The traps to avoid.

  • Not Gen-Z-only. We're a mix of Graza (everyone, any age/body/gender) and Poppi (30s-60s, still aspirational). We keep Type C, and add the fun-in-life layer.
  • Not mean. The villain is the "supposed-to" and the chore, never people who iron.
  • Not corporate or over-polished. Warm, real, a bit imperfect. Raw beats glossy.
  • Not just demos. The odd demo is fine, but entertainment and identity carry the brand.
  • Not smug. Keep the fun inclusive (the Poppi vending-machine lesson), never a flex that leaves people out.
