How this shoot works

One persona, one angle, one body, then its hooks.

A hook is a promise. If the hook says "between open homes" and the body is a generic demo, the ad never pays the promise back and the viewer drops. So hooks are not a shared bank. Each one belongs to the body it was written for.

Persona→Angle→Body→Its own hooks
Six clusters, 35 hooks. Reuse happens inside a cluster, not across it: film one body and six openers and you have six ads that all hold together. Six clusters gets you to roughly 35 finished ads off one studio day, which covers 20 to 30 a week for over a month. The variable being tested stays clean, because within a cluster the only thing moving is the first three seconds.

The one rule that matters: a hook never moves to another cluster. If a hook feels like it belongs somewhere else, that is a sign the cluster needs a different body, not that the hook should travel.

The launch videos already work this way, which is why USA, UK and NZ are one item each rather than three. Each shares a single body with three openers on the front.

⚑ Rules that make the pieces actually cut together
  • Shoot a whole cluster in one run. Body first, then its hooks back to back, same outfit, same background, same camera position. Move on only when the cluster is finished.
  • Redress between clusters, not within one. Cluster 4 needs to look like a small apartment and cluster 3 needs a clock and movement, so they cannot share a setup.
  • Never name a colour or a garment in a hook, in case the body shows a different one.
  • No claims in a hook. Claims live in the body, so each one gets written and checked once.
  • End each hook clean. No trailing word, no answering your own setup. The body answers it.
  • Start every body already mid-action so a hook cuts straight in with no dead beat.
  • Shoot each hook twice: once down the lens, once already moving.
  • Say the hook number out loud before each take and record 30 seconds of room tone. Both save hours in the edit.
  • No "in seconds" or any timing claim. No em dashes on screen. No "made in Australia", say Australian brand.

The six clusters

Cluster 1 · body

Gets changed at work

Persona

Alex, the office professional. Corporate or client-facing, carries a change of clothes, gets dressed somewhere that is not home.

Angle

The shirt was fine when you folded it. It is not fine when it comes out of the bag, and there is no iron anywhere near you.

What makes the hooks and this body match
The demo in this body must be on a shirt pulled straight out of a bag with hard fold lines across it. Not a generically scrunched shirt. Every hook promises the bag moment, so the body has to show the bag moment.
Body script

You get to work, pull the shirt out of your bag, and it has that fold line straight across the middle.

So now you either wear it like that, or you go looking for an iron in an office that definitely does not have one.

(pull shirt from bag, spray, smooth, hold up)

This lives in my bag. Non-toxic, and it washes out in the next wash.

Thirty day money back if it is not for you.

Props
Work bag or tote. Two shirts folded into it hard the night before so the creases are real. Bottle.

Cluster 2 · body

Living out of a suitcase

Persona

Sienna, the traveller. Also anyone whose job puts them in hotels. Travel is the number one theme in your reviews.

Angle

You are not going to iron on holiday, and you were never going to. The hotel iron exists to be ignored.

What makes the hooks and this body match
The body has to open on an actual suitcase being opened and a genuinely destroyed linen shirt coming out of it. If the hook promises the suitcase and the body starts on a hanger, it breaks.
Body script

Every hotel room has an iron and a board in the wardrobe.

I have never once used one, and neither have you.

(open case, pull out crushed linen shirt, spray, smooth, put it on)

It goes in the bag, it comes out, and I get dressed. That is the whole system.

Works on cotton, linen and viscose. Not polyester.

Then
Tail A, shop the link below.
Props
Open suitcase, packed. Crushed linen shirt and linen pants. Travel bottle. Full bottle.

Cluster 3 · body

The morning has no spare twenty minutes

Persona

Mia, the working mum, and anyone whose morning is already full before they get dressed. Core persona, and the biggest audience.

Angle

It is not that you cannot iron. It is that the twenty minutes does not exist and something has to give.

What makes the hooks and this body match
Time pressure has to be visible in the body: the clock, the half-packed bag, moving while you talk. Never stationary at a clean backdrop, because every hook in this cluster promises a morning in progress.
Body script

It is 8:14. Everyone needs something from me and I am not dressed.

The shirt I planned to wear has been in a ball since the weekend.

Old me irons it and is late. Current me does this.

(spray, smooth, put it on, keep moving)

Out the door, and nobody can tell.

Then
Tail A, shop the link below.
Props
Visible clock or phone showing the time. Half-packed bag. Crumpled shirt. Bottle. Shoot handheld and moving.
No kids on camera. The pressure is carried by the clock, the bag and the pace, not by another person in frame.

Cluster 4 · body

Nowhere to put an ironing board

Persona

Dani, the apartment dweller. Small place, laundry is a cupboard, storage is the constraint. Currently the least served persona in the account.

Angle

This is not about hating ironing. It is that the equipment does not fit in your life, physically.

What makes the hooks and this body match
The body needs to be shot somewhere visibly small: a door, a rail, a narrow hallway. A big clean studio backdrop kills the entire premise.
Body script

Where exactly am I supposed to put an ironing board.

My laundry is a cupboard. There is no spare wall, there is no spare corner.

So the shirt gets hung on the back of the door and sprayed there.

(spray on the hanging shirt, smooth, take it down)

One bottle. That is the entire setup.

Then
Tail C, silent product card.
Props
A door with a hook, or a clothing rail. Hanger. Crumpled shirt. Bottle. Dress the corner of the studio so it reads small and domestic.

Cluster 5 · body

You think this does not work

Persona

Not a life stage, a state of mind. Someone who has already bought a wrinkle spray that did nothing, or assumes all of them are water in a bottle.

Angle

Do not argue. Show one garment, one variable, and admit the limitation before they find it.

What makes the hooks and this body match
This body is the half and half proof, so every hook here has to be a challenge or a doubt. A warm lifestyle hook on the front of this body wastes it.
Body script

One shirt. I am spraying this half and leaving that half alone.

(spray left half on camera, lay flat, one unbroken pan across both halves)

Same shirt, same minute, same light. That is the difference.

It will not do this on polyester, and I would rather tell you now than have you find out.

Thirty day money back.

Then
Tail B, thirty day money back.
Props
One cotton or linen shirt, crumpled evenly across both halves. Flat surface with even light edge to edge. Bottle.
Even light across the whole garment matters more than anything else in this cluster. One shadow on one half and the proof reads as a trick.

Cluster 6 · body

Why it exists at all

Persona

Cold audience who has never heard of you. The founder story is doing the work of a review here.

Angle

A person made this because she refused to iron, and kept going until it actually worked.

What makes the hooks and this body match
This body is you talking, so the hooks all have to be first person and low status. An audience-callout hook on the front of a founder body is a mismatch, because the ad never returns to that audience.
Body script

I hated ironing, so I tried everything that was already out there. All of it was useless or as much work as just ironing.

Halfway through, my dad was diagnosed with cancer, and that changed what I was willing to put on my clothes.

So I reformulated the whole thing to be non-toxic.

Sixteen versions later I had it. (demo cutaway)

Now it ships all over Australia.

Then
Tail C, silent, or Tail B.
Props
You, bottle in hand. Clean backdrop. Body 1 demo footage covers the cutaway.
Confirm the order count and the months before any of this ships. The plan currently carries both 10,000 in seven months and 13,000 in eight months.

Tails · film once, use everywhere

Set piece

The skit, "give it here"

DemoTwo people (A + B)Objection handling
Reference
Lash-extensions talking-to-self ref video ↗
Setting
B is ready, waiting. A walks in with a wrinkled shirt, flustered.
Script

A: Sorry, give me another 20 minutes, I just need to iron this.

B: Iron? Give it here.

A: (skeptical) Wait... does that even actually work?

B: (spraying) Watch. (start demo)

A: Okay but I don't want chemicals sitting on my clothes all day.

B: (still smoothing) Non-toxic. Washes straight out next wash.

A: Is it gonna be all wet though?

B: (handing it back) Not really, and any small wet patches dry quickly.

A: (surprised, holding it up) ...Okay that's actually smooth.

B: (grabbing the bottle back) Get your own. This one's mine.

Props
Wrinkled shirt, Uncrumpled bottle.
Needs a second person on camera. Your no-characters rule is for your organic page, so confirm you are happy with someone in this one.
Set piece

Only good use for an iron

ComedyStudio friendlyNew
Straight-faced product review of an iron, for everything except clothes. Toastie press. Sealing a chip packet. Reheating last night's pizza. Score it out of ten as a kitchen appliance.
How to shoot it

Deliver it completely deadpan, like a genuine review.

Three uses maximum, the fourth stops being funny.

End by putting it away in a cupboard, not in a bin. It is not a funeral.

Props
Iron, bread and cheese, a chip packet, a plate. Baking paper so you do not wreck the iron.
This is not the iron funeral idea, which you killed. Nothing gets destroyed and nothing gets mourned.
Set piece

Reading real one star reviews of irons

ComedyPhone onlySeries
Other people's words, read off your phone, straight face. Category attack with none of the risk, because you are not the one making the accusation.
How to shoot it

Pull genuine one star reviews of irons and steamers from retail sites.

Read four or five. Do not editorialise, the reviews are funnier than a comment.

End on the bottle with no line at all.

Props
Phone, bottle.
Read reviews of the category, never of a named competitor product. Screenshot nothing with a brand name visible.
Set piece

Does it work on...

SeriesComment baitHighest volume
Escalating items. A tote bag. A pillowcase. A fitted sheet. A tablecloth. A shirt while it is still on you. Every comment suggesting another item is the next episode.
How to shoot it

One item per cut so each becomes its own ad.

Include one that does not work. It is the only reason anyone believes the ones that do.

Ask for the next item at the end.

Props
Tote, pillowcase, fitted sheet, tablecloth, a shirt you can wear.
Set piece

Uncrumpled versus the internet's hacks

ComparisonStudio friendlyLeaderboard
Test the free workarounds people already use: hairdryer, ice cubes in the dryer, damp towel, hanging it in the shower, sitting on it. Rank them worst to best, land on the spray.
How to shoot it

Same garment type for every test so the comparison holds.

Let the bad ones fail on camera without narrating the failure.

Leaderboard graphic goes on in the edit.

Props
Hairdryer, ice, damp towel, five matching crumpled shirts, bottle.
Do not put a time on any of them, including yours.

Script

Hey. I wanted to make this one just to say thank you.

When I started Uncrumpled, it was just me, a spray bottle, and a real hatred of ironing. Honestly, I had no idea if anyone else would care.

And then you did.

For a while there, I packed every single order myself and handwrote a thank-you card to go in each one. I did that until my early-onset arthritis meant I couldn't anymore.

I still reply to every email. Every DM, every comment, that's all still me.

I'm so thankful. I genuinely love working on this business, and I'm so grateful you're supporting it.

Set piece

What if you never needed to iron?

To cameraFounder VSLLong form
The long-form version. Bold lines are delivered to camera, the rest can sit over demo cutaways. This one stays whole, but the opening line also works as hook 17 if you want it both ways.
Script

What if you never actually needed to iron?

One day I noticed something. When you want to get the wrinkles out of a shirt, everyone tells you the same thing: drag out the board, heat up the iron, wait around, press every inch. For one creased top. Or buy a steamer, fill the tank, wait for it to heat up, hold the steamer on it, but don't hold it too close or it might burn, and don't forget to empty the water afterwards or mould will grow.

Do I seriously need a whole appliance and half my morning just to look put together?

Now what if someone who genuinely hated ironing came along and made a spray purely to get wrinkles out. Something that wasn't just water and fragrance in a bottle. No heat, no board, obsessed with actually working and being non-toxic enough to use every single morning. Wouldn't that be good?

I thought so. So that's what I did.

Uncrumpled started from my hatred of ironing, so I decided to do something about it. I tried everything out there, all useless or as much work as just ironing. Then my dad got diagnosed with cancer, and I reformulated the whole thing to be non-toxic and quick-drying.

Sixteen samples later I had it, and now I'm shipping it all over Australia with a ton of reviews from people who never want to touch an iron again.

So if you like the sound of getting the wrinkles out with just a spray and not much else, check it out. Plus, we're so confident in it that if you don't love it, we'll give you your money back.

Props
You to camera plus a demo cutaway to drop over the "made a spray" beat. Body 1 covers the cutaway.
