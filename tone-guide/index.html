We are the friend who is also running late. Fun, sassy, playful, relatable chaos.

The 4 traits, and where each one stops

Fun

We have fun with what we do, and it shows. High energy, a bit of chaos, personality in everything. We show the fun behind the scenes, not just the polished front. We are not a boring brand with no personality.

The limit. Fun is not wacky and it is not a string of gag lines. It is energy and personality. If it reads like a brand trying to be funny, it is not us.

It takes you 3 to 5 business days to respond to a text. That's how we knew Uncrumpled had to be quick.

Sassy

Little one-liners. At the iron, or about who we are. Confident, honest, a bit cheeky. Think Frank Body: "You'll be naked soon", "Let's be frank", honest reviews on the packaging.

The limit. Sass is cheeky, never mean. It lands on the iron, the chore, the brand or Ann. If a line would sting a tired mum at 8:14am, cut it.

Uncrumpled replaces the iron you never use.

Playful

We take little jabs. Not proper jabs, just "you take 3 days to answer a text" jabs. "For the lazy girl" said with a wink, never as an insult. We are laughing with her, because we are her.

The limit. Playful is a tone, not a gimmick. No "show us your chair" community stunts. The play is in the words.

Pick those pants off the floor, smooth the creases, wear them for 3 days straight. Uncrumpled doesn't judge.

Relatable chaos

We show the real life behind what people post. The messy house, the 6,000 unread emails, the shirt on the floor. We understand being too busy to iron, too busy to clean, too busy full stop. We are out there and we bring the chaos with us.

The limit. Chaos, not Certified Chaos. Relatable, not a lecture. We never tell her she should have it together. She knows.

We're here to smooth over life's little wrinkles.

Who we are talking to

Type C. She wants to be organised. She has the planner, the meal prep containers, the Sunday reset. She just cannot mentally get there, and by Wednesday it has all fallen over. We do not laugh at her. We are her.

  • Meal prep Sunday. Uber Eats Wednesday.
  • Cleaned the wardrobe Sunday. Clothes on the chair by Monday.
  • Has a schedule written out in full. Has never followed it.
  • Sets 5 alarms. Snoozes all 5.
  • Sends "on my way" from the shower.
  • Owns an iron. Has not touched it since the move.
  • 6,000 unread emails. Not stressed about it. A bit stressed about it.
  • Bought the linen dress. Wears the same 3 outfits.

Rachel Green

Rachel Green from Friends. Chaos, but fashionable. Put together on the outside, scrambling underneath. Charming, always slightly late, cares a lot about how she looks and not at all about the boring work of getting there. Funny by being honest about it, never bitter. If you know the show, that is the register.

Write as one of us

Write as one of us, never as the observer. "We" and "I" put us in the room with her. "Busy women like you" puts us behind a desk.

Type C, chaos, and not Type B

Type C is the label and it is fine in copy. "Chaos" is fine as a word. We are not using "Certified Chaos" for now. Never "Type B": that is the chill one who does not try and gets away with it, and it is a different lane.

What we sound like

We sound like

  • A mate texting you from the car park, not a brand posting.
  • Short sentences. Full stops. The rhythm is fragments.
  • One plain statement, then one small shrug. "I don't iron anymore. Highly recommend."
  • Honest. We say what it does, what it does not do, and what we actually think.
  • High energy. Personality in the caption, the reply, the packaging, the boring bits.
  • Aussie. "Bloody", "reckon", "cbf" all fine on organic and email. Never in a paid ad.
  • First person. Ann started this, Ann still has not ironed, Ann is in the copy.
  • Cheeky. The funniest line is delivered with a straight face.

We never sound like

  • A skincare brand that discovered a pun. No "wrinkle-free wonder", no "crease crusader".
  • A lecture. Never tell her she should iron less, or should have it together. She knows.
  • A science lab. No formula, technology, innovative, solution, superior, professional results. CreaseDrop is the one exception, it is ours.
  • A cheerleader. No "you've got this queen", no "yasss", no motivational poster energy.
  • Two jokes in one line. If it needs a second image to land, it is tryhard. Cut one.
  • Mean. Never "lazy" as an insult, never "messy" as a diagnosis. "For the lazy girl" only ever with a wink.
  • Perfect. No immaculate, pristine, spotless, flawless, domestic goddess. That is the enemy.
  • Boring. A plain product fact with no personality attached is every other brand.
