uncrumpled.

Less pretty.
More real.

The old look was calm, cream and tidy. The new one is a flash photo taken at the wrong moment in a house that looks like ours. People in it. Mess as the set. The spray turns up the way lip balm turns up: in the bag, on the counter, in the back pocket, never posed in the middle.

Every image on this page is generated as a reference for the feeling. None of it is final. Reshoot all of it with real people, real houses, real mess. If a frame looks too clean when you shoot it, it is too clean.
01 · The shift

From product demo to certified chaos

Same brand, same green, same spray. The camera just stops pretending we have our lives together.

Where we have been
  • Cream, calm, editorial. Soft daylight, tidy bedroom, one shirt.
  • The product demo is the whole video. Spray, smooth, reveal.
  • Nobody in frame, or one person alone with the bottle.
  • Slow, considered, a bit precious about the clothes.
  • Reads as a nice ad. Does not read as a person.
Where we are going
  • Flash on. Harsh, saturated, slightly overexposed, deep shadows. The 2008 digicam look.
  • The mess is the set. Chair pile, bag tip-out, bed of outfits, cereal at 11pm.
  • People, plural. Friends mid laugh, mid shout, mid hop. Caught, not posed.
  • The spray is an accessory. It is in the scene at position three, never the hero of the frame.
  • Reads like a mate's camera roll. Funny because nobody is reacting to the chaos.
02 · The reference, and what we take from it

Good Girl Snacks is the energy. We are not the hot girl version.

They made a pickle jar a fashion accessory with flash photography and a joke nobody explains. That mechanic is exactly right for us. The register is not: they are Gen Z, hot pink, going out. We are early thirties, green, running late.

Take
  • Direct flash as the whole visual identity. One camera setting does the branding.
  • Product as an accessory, held like a drink, carried like lip balm, never demonstrated.
  • Self aware fun. Nobody winks. The joke is that it is treated as completely normal.
  • People in every frame, friends not models, real bodies, real faces.
  • Saturated colour and bold captions in the brand's own voice.
Leave
  • Hot girl, pink, bows, nightclub. Our girl is on the airport floor, not the podium.
  • Gen Z casting. Ours are around thirty with a job, a kid or a flight.
  • Styled mess. Their chaos is art directed. Ours has to be an actual house.
  • Any colour swap. Green stays the anchor. No orange, no terracotta, no pink takeover.
03 · Six rules of the look

If a shot breaks one of these, it is the old brand

1

Flash on. Always.

Phone flash or a cheap compact camera with the flash forced on. Slightly overexposed skin, hard shadows on the wall behind. No portrait mode, no filters, no grain overlays. The flash is the filter.

2

The mess is the set.

Do not tidy before shooting. Do not stage a mess either, it always looks staged. Shoot the real chair pile, the real bag, the real counter. If the house is clean that day, shoot somewhere else.

3

Caught, not posed.

Mid laugh, mid hop, mid sentence, eyes closed, blinking. Take twenty frames and keep the one where nobody was ready. Motion blur is allowed. Symmetry is not.

4

Spray at position three.

The bottle is one object in a scene of many. On the brunch table between the sunglasses and the salt. In the bag tip-out. Sticking out of a pocket. It is never centred, never on a clean background, never the reveal.

5

Green is the anchor.

The sage bottle is the one consistent colour across every frame. Everything else can be loud: denim, tiles, neon, wine. No orange, no terracotta, no pink takeover. Green plus chaos is the palette.

6

Caption in our voice.

Big white meme bar, lime sticker, or handwritten scrawl. Short, deadpan, first person. The caption admits the thing. It never explains the product and never says a time it takes.

05 · Text on the image

Three treatments, rotate them

All three sit on top of the flash photo. Never a full colour block, never a gradient, never a serif on the image. The serif is for this document, not for the feed.

The meme bar

Iron pile Sunday. Spray and walk Monday. Same.

White bar across the top, black heavy sans, centred. Reads as a screenshot someone sent you. Best for the headline pattern: the aspirational thing, the honest thing, then Same.

The sticker

Not a chair. A system.

Lime pill, rotated a few degrees, bottom left or wherever the mess is not. Short, four to six words. Use it when the photo already says everything and the sticker is just the punchline.

The scrawl

tomorrow is sorted

Handwriting, white, a thin black glow so it survives any photo. Lowercase. Reads as a note to self. Use it on the quieter night-time frames, the kitchen, the laundry mountain, the couch.

Caption bank to pull from
Meal prep Sunday. Uber Eats Wednesday. Same. 8am alarm. 8:14 panic. Same. Folded laundry basket. Chair pile. Same. Inbox zero. 14,372 unread. Same. Not a chair. A system. Wore it yesterday. Nobody knows. Packed in 9 minutes. Regrets. Certified Chaos. Spray. Smooth. Done. You're not lazy. You're optimised.
06 · Where the bottle lives

In the scene, never in front of it

Yes, it lives here

  • Sticking out of a tote, a back pocket, a coat pocket.
  • On the bathroom counter between the mascara and the cold coffee.
  • On the brunch table, the kitchen bench, the coffee table, the car seat.
  • In someone's hand mid conversation, being passed to a friend, used on a friend.
  • In the bag tip-out, item nine of twenty.
  • On the floor next to the chair pile, on the pillow next to a face down human.

No, never here

  • Centred on a clean background with nothing else in frame.
  • Held up to camera and presented like a shopping channel.
  • As the final reveal after a build up. It is a beat, not a climax.
  • Tidy before, messy after, or any side by side comparison. Side by side is dead.
  • Being sprayed on anything delicate: silk, suede, vintage, heirloom, anything that looks precious.
  • In more than about two thirds of frames. Some frames have no bottle. That is what makes it a look and not a catalogue.
07 · How to shoot it

One house, one night out, one day

Every frame on this page comes from seven locations and a handful of props. Shoot stills and 9:16 video from every setup, the same setup is a post and a reel.

Locations and setups

  • Bedroom: bed covered in outfits with two people (01), the chair pile (02), laundry mountain (10).
  • Bathroom: counter chaos and mirror selfie (03).
  • Hallway and front door: the running late hop (04).
  • Kitchen: 11pm cereal at the open fridge (14), the bag tip-out on the table (08).
  • Desk or couch: work from home waist up (11), couch with wine and tomorrow's outfits (16).
  • Out: back seat of a car at night (05), a cafe table overhead (06), a shop change room (15), an airport gate floor (07).
  • Night: front steps group shot (12), one dancing frame in a dark room (09).

Bring and settings

  • Phone with flash forced ON, or a cheap compact digital camera. Flash on for every frame including daylight.
  • No portrait mode, no night mode, no filters. Slight overexposure is right. Fix nothing in the edit except crop.
  • Three to five friends who will sign a release and do not mind looking mid blink.
  • Four or five sage bottles so one is in every scene without moving it.
  • Linen shirts, cotton dresses, jeans. Creased on arrival. No silk, no delicates.
  • Real props already in the house: tote, wine glasses, cereal, cold coffee, hair tool, receipts, keys.
  • Twenty frames per setup minimum. Keep the ones where someone was not ready.
08 · Hard no

The fast ways to end up back in the old brand

Never

  • Natural light, soft focus, clean bedroom. That is the old look.
  • Staged mess. If it was arranged for the camera it reads as arranged.
  • Gowns, occasion wear, anything that looks like a special event outfit.
  • Vintage, heirloom, silk, suede, anything precious near the bottle.
  • Split screens or before and after. Contrast lives in the caption, not the frame.
  • Orange, terracotta, pink as a brand colour. Green anchors, the world provides the rest.
  • Film grain or a vintage filter added in the edit. The flash already did it.
  • Explaining the product in the caption, or saying how long it takes.

Always

  • Flash on.
  • Somebody in frame, ideally more than one.
  • Something that should have been tidied and was not.
  • The bottle in the scene, small, in about two of every three frames.
  • A caption that admits the thing rather than sells the thing.
  • Cotton or linen on every body.
  • Shoot the still and the vertical video from the same setup before moving on.
