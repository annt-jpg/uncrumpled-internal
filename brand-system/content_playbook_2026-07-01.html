01 / The north star

We're not selling a spray. We're giving people permission to live their authentic life.

Everyone already has permission to live. What they don't give themselves is permission to skip the mundane, the polite, the perfect, and live the messy, authentic, full-to-them life they actually want. Do what you want. Skip what you don't. Don't waste time on the things that won't matter in five years.

The villain

The life you're "supposed" to live

Ironing. Waking up to pressure. Chores that no longer serve a purpose. Being polite and perfect. A spotless house, always. Home-cooked meals every single night, zero screen time, the influencer-perfect life. The life you're told you should want.

vs
The hero

The life that's actually yours

Brunches, travel, sunsets, dinners at the table. Being gloriously imperfect and doing it anyway. Skipping the chores that don't need doing. A messy, real, authentic life. And uncrumpling a shirt instead of ironing it.

It's not that you don't care how you look. You just refuse to waste your life ironing to get there. Spray it and go live.

What every video is really saying

"You have better things to do than iron. Here's your way out." We're not anti looking good, we're anti wasting your life to get there. Uncrumpled is the shortcut: the sorted shirt in 3 seconds, so the time goes to living. The product is the exit door, never the whole story.

The lines

Less ironing. More living. is the house line, on everything, and it's the shape of every video too. Life's too short to iron. is the hero line for campaigns and big brand moments. Spray. Smooth. Go. is the product mechanic. Underneath all three: do what you want, skip what you don't.

02 / The voice

Fun, real, a bit cheeky, occasionally tender. Never corporate, never preachy.

The blend: Type C relatable chaos, mixed with "sorry Nan, we're not ironing on this trip," mixed with a rebellious "forget what you're supposed to do, go live" energy. But we can also be quiet and real about the moments that matter. Range is the point.

We are
  • Fun and a little bit naughty. We poke at the rules.
  • Real and imperfect. Messy houses, honest confessions, no filter.
  • Warm and human. A founder-mum who hates ironing talking to people who hate ironing.
  • Aspirational in an ordinary way. Brunch and travel and family, not private jets.
  • Sometimes tender. The moments. Time with the kids. What actually matters.
  • Confident and quick. We get to the point and we make you feel something fast.
We are not
  • Corporate, polished or "brand-safe" boring.
  • Preachy or lecturing. We never tell people how to live, we show a more fun option.
  • Trying to be perfect. Perfect is the enemy. Raw beats glossy here, always.
  • Mean or negative for the sake of it. Cheeky, not bitter.
  • Promoting getting blind drunk. The energy is fun and free, the payoff varies.
  • A product demo pretending to be content.
03 / The content pillars

Everything we make is one of these shapes.

You never start from a blank page. You pick a pillar and refill it. The mess and the chaos are always the setup, never the whole video. They always turn into a laugh, a hack, or a win.

1

The mess

The heart · why people feel seen

Type C confessions. The chaos, the imperfect house, the "it's fine to be like this." This is what makes people feel understood. But relatable alone gets a nod and a scroll, so it always needs a strong hook and something happening: a reveal, a hack, or a punchline that keeps people watching. Mostly founder-made (Ann's own POV), not paid-creator work. (Still to hash out: exactly how these look so people binge, not just relate.)

2

Less ironing, more living

The hero · the whole point

Our biggest pillar, and it merges the product with the promise. Never a boring "watch me uncrumple this shirt" demo. It's the anti-iron moment and the life it buys you, shown together in a fun, relatable way. Think grandma's holiday vs yours, or the ironing math: the spray is in there, but the story is about what you get to do instead. Demo plus the living, always.

3

Real ones

The trust · the human behind it

Wins and losses from the founder. Building in the open. This works only when it opens with a specific grenade, not "hey guys, let me tell you about my week." People fall for the human before they fall for the brand.

4

The community

The world · proof it's real

Real customers' real lives, reposted. Reviews, messy-real clips, and eventually events and giving back. This is how the brand becomes bigger than one person. It's also our free, sustainable UGC engine (see section 06).

5

The moments

The why · the deeper end

The quiet, real, tender end of the brand. Time with the kids while they're young. Dinners at the table. What you actually get back when you stop wasting life on chores that won't matter. This is where the emotional content lives (the manifesto idea in the idea bank sits here). It gives the brand somewhere deep to go, not just cheeky.

04 / The structure

Hook, then less ironing, more living.

Not a rigid formula. A simple shape that turns the tagline into the video: grab attention, show the product doing its thing without dwelling on the demo, then land on the life it bought back. The tagline is the structure.

First 2 seconds
Hook

The job: stop the scroll and make them stay. Usually just bold text on screen. A question, a bold claim, or a "vs" set-up they need to see resolved.

Looks like
  • "The holiday Nan thinks I had vs the holiday I had."
  • "That 25 minutes you spent ironing? Here's what you could've done instead."
  • "POV: getting ready in 3 seconds, not 30 minutes."
Then
Less ironing (or more living)*

The product doing its thing, quick and light. Never a laboured demo. A fast spray, or the boring "supposed to" version we're skipping. In and out.

Looks like
  • one spray, out the door
  • Nan's version: iron the blouse, high tea, prim, in bed by 9
  • the 30-second get-ready instead of the 30-minute one
Then
More living (or less ironing)*

The point. What the product bought you: the time, the energy, the life. Land here, this is the emotional hit and the reason to share.

Looks like
  • the sleep-in you got instead
  • brunch, dancing, sunsets, the holiday you actually had
  • coffee in peace, playing with the kids, doing nothing at all
* Not a fixed order. The hook always comes first. After that, less ironing and more living go in either order, whatever the video needs. Lead with the living, or end on it. This is the shape, not a formula.

Hook"The holiday Nan thinks I had vs the holiday I had." (text on screen)

The 2-second test

Does the first 2 seconds make someone need to know what happens next? Or can they agree and keep scrolling? If they can scroll, the hook is dead. Rewrite it.

05 / Idea bank

The ideas.

Here are some ideas that we had and that work well. We would love for you, as the creator, to come up with things that feel native to you, while following the structure and brand personality set out in this document. These are the selling ideas: demo-light and living-forward, and most need only a clip or two plus text plus a trending sound. Don't over-produce.

Grandma's holiday vs yours

Less ironing, more living

The generational three-way. What Nan thinks a holiday is versus what yours actually is. The comedy is in the gap. Very taggable.

On-screen "Nan's holiday packing list: travel iron, board cover, spray starch, 3 pressed blouses, in bed by 9." → "My packing list: Uncrumpled. One spray. Done." → cut to you at brunch / on a boat / laughing. Caption: "Sorry Nan, the iron stayed home."

The ironing math

Less ironing, more living

Trade the time back. The minutes you spend ironing, reframed as the fun you could have had instead. Rotate the payoff so it's not one-note.

Hook "That 25 minutes you spent ironing? Here's what you could've done instead." → rapid cuts: a sleep-in / a real breakfast / two cocktails at the airport bar / the sunset you missed / a proper chat with your kid → spray the shirt → "Uncrumpled. Go live."

Less of this. More of this.

Less ironing, more living

A split or hard cut between the boring life and the good life. Fast, rhythmic, set to a track. Works as an organic post and a paid ad.

Structure Less of this: ironing board, fluoro light, flat. More of this: beach, brunch, dancing, spraying a shirt in a hotel mirror. Text-light, sound-led.

Things your holiday does NOT need

Less ironing, more living

A cheeky list of the boring stuff you can leave behind, landing on the one thing you should bring.

On-screen "Things your holiday does NOT need: an iron. A 6am alarm to press a shirt. Spray starch. A lecture from your mother." → "Things it DOES need:" (hold up Uncrumpled).

The 4-minute reset

The mess

Chaos plus a clock plus a spray-and-go payoff. The mess is the setup, the reset is the reward.

On-screen "POV: someone just texted 'here in 5' and my house looks like THIS." → frantic chaos → "watch the reset" → speed-run tidy, last move is spraying the crushed shirt off the chair → door opens, you look flawless.

The torture test, done right

Less ironing, more living

A demo that works, because it withholds the result. Never announce the ending in the first frame.

Hook "This shirt's been shoved in a gym bag for 3 days. No iron in the house. One spray." → hold up the disaster, do NOT show the result yet → beat → the reveal → out the door.

The founder grenade

Real ones

Talk-to-camera that people actually watch, because line one is a specific, honest bomb (and it's on screen as text too).

Hook "I spent $400 on one video and it got 12 likes. Here's what that taught me about wasting money as a founder." → the lesson. Face is fine, the first line does the work.

The swap

Less ironing, more living

Then versus now. The chore you used to do, replaced with the life you do now.

On-screen "This used to be my Sunday." (ironing pile) → "Now it's this." (kids / beach / coffee).

The ADHD spiral, with a landing

The mess

The relatable brain-tangent format, but it must resolve into a punchline that ties back to us. A spiral that goes nowhere flops.

Hook "Watch my brain try to hang up ONE shirt." → chaotic tangent montage → snaps back → "…40 minutes later, here's the one shirt. At least I sprayed it instead of ironing it."

The two holidays

Less ironing, more living

The holiday Nan pictured versus the one you actually had. Prim expectation, then the real, fun, sprayed-and-out-the-door version.

On-screen "The holiday Nan thinks I had" (iron the blouse, high tea, prim, in bed by 9) → "vs the holiday I had" (sprayed it, out the door, brunch, dancing, sunsets).

Less ___, more ___ (everyday)

Less ironing, more living

The everyday version, no travel needed. Swap the chore for an ordinary good thing. Endlessly repeatable, one clip each.

On-screen "Less ironing. More ___" → sleeping in / coffee in peace / time with the kids / walking the dog / doing absolutely nothing. Quick spray, then the good bit.

What it bought me

Less ironing, more living

Sell the time and energy saved. Open on the number, land on the living.

Hook "I haven't ironed in a year. Here's what I did with all that time." → a montage of ordinary living (mornings, kids, coffee, sun) → "Uncrumpled. Less ironing, more living."

Less of the bad, more of the good

Less ironing, more living

The tagline as a whole life philosophy. A rhythmic list that lands on us, and positions the brand as bigger than a spray.

On-screen "Less doom-scrolling, more sunsets. Less pretending, more living. Less ironing… you get it." Fast cuts, trending sound.

Cool Grandma

Less ironing, more living

Flip the grandma stereotype. Everyone expects the ironing, the high tea, prim and proper. Instead she sprays her shirt and rides off doing cool-grandma stuff. Living, at any age.

On-screen "What Nan's supposed to be doing:" (ironing, high tea, in bed by 9) → "What Nan's actually doing:" one spray, throws a leg over a motorbike, rides off.
Concepts to develop
Idea 01 · the manifesto ad

"Don't waste your life on things that won't matter. Bad people. And ironing."

Leans into the tender, real end of the brand (the "moments" pillar), then undercuts it with the cheeky anti-iron punch. The "things that won't matter" list can flex (bad people, toxic jobs, doom-scrolling, ironing). Lands on: spray it, go live. This one could carry the whole positioning as a hero ad. Needs the full list and a visual treatment worked out.

06 / Where the content comes from

You already have more than you think. Raw beats glossy.

The "living" is not jetskis and bungee jumps. It's brunch, the sunset, the kids in the pool, a messy night out. Ordinary real life, filmed on a phone, is the aesthetic. Do not set the bar at "epic" and then feel unable to clear it.

1

The camera roll Free · now

Years of trips, dinners, kids, beaches and nights out are already sitting in the phone. That's the first two weeks of content, already shot. Pull 10 "living" clips and pair each with an ironing clip plus a text hook.

2

Capture as you go Free · habit

Grab a few 5-second clips at every outing. Not "making content", just filming the life you're already living. In a month there's more than you can post.

3

The community engine Free · scalable

The big one, and it replaces expensive paid UGC. Prompt customers: "Spray it and show us what you did instead of ironing." Repost the best. More authentic than anything staged, and it builds the community pillar at the same time.

4

Stitch and react Free · fast

Green-screen or react to clips already going viral (a girls trip, a travel fail, a Type C confession). Native, fast, and rides existing reach.

5

Stock, only for the gaps

The true jetski or a country you're not in. Free commercial stock lives on Pexels and Pixabay. Use it sparingly and rough so it never looks stocky. Real always wins.

The bar-lowering truth

The best versions of these ideas are often one clip plus text on screen plus a trending sound. You do not need a montage. "The 25 minutes you spent ironing…" over a single sunset clip with the right audio beats a produced edit. Stop picturing a production.

07 / Guardrails

The lines that keep us on-brand and out of trouble.

Non-negotiables for anyone writing or editing. Break these and the content is off-brand or non-compliant.

Alcohol: organic vs paid. On organic you can be as cheeky as you like. On paid Meta ads, alcohol references throttle reach and targeting, so for ads lean the payoff toward fun-not-booze (a sleep-in, an extra swim, time with the kids). Keep the attitude, swap the shot. We never promote getting blind drunk.

No em dashes. Use a hyphen with spaces ( - ) or a comma. This is a hard brand rule in all Uncrumpled copy.

Sentence case and Australian English. Organise, colour, favourite. Capitalise only the first word and proper nouns in headlines.

Say "Australian-owned", never "Aussie-made" or "made in Australia". The brand is Perth-owned. Do not claim local manufacturing.

Never promise "perfect" or "professional" finish. It smooths most everyday wrinkles fast. It will not beat a professional iron's razor-sharp finish, and the site already says so. Don't oversell it.

No polyester or synthetic claims, and no deep-set / dry-clean-only crease claims. The product works best on everyday fabrics like cotton, linen and viscose. Don't set an expectation it can't meet.

No medical or therapeutic claims. Don't name competitor brands. Skip "eco" / "green". Stay clean-ingredient adjacent (free from formaldehyde, phthalates, parabens) rather than making environmental claims.

09 / How to use this

Your quick start.

Creators: start with a pillar and an idea

Pick a pillar (section 03), pick an idea (section 05), and run it through hook-turn-payoff (section 04). Most need one clip plus text plus a sound. Raw beats glossy, always.

Designers & editors: protect the hook

The first 2 seconds and the on-screen text carry the video. Make the hook text bold and instant. Withhold the payoff. Keep the vibe fun and imperfect, never over-polished.

Agencies: match the voice, respect the guardrails

Read section 02 (voice) and section 07 (guardrails) before writing a word. If a line sounds corporate, cut it. If it breaks a guardrail, it doesn't ship.

Everyone: the 2-second test

Before you post, ask: does the first 2 seconds make someone need to know what happens, or can they nod and scroll? If they can scroll, rework the hook.
