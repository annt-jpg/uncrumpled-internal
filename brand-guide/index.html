01Who we are

We are the friend who is also running late. Fun, sassy, playful, relatable chaos.

Nobody shows off a spray bottle

People collect clothes, makeup and bags. Nobody collects anti-crease spray. So we do not sell the spray on socials. We run the Type C Club: a feed for the chaotically busy, slightly over-scheduled and done chasing domestic perfection. The Chair, panic packing, the 8:14 hallway. The spray shows up because it lives in her life, not because it is the point of the post.

02How we talk

Four traits. Every line should hit at least one. The full tone guide has the whole one-liner bank; this is the short version.

Fun

High energy, personality in everything, the fun behind the scenes. Not gag lines. A brand that is not boring.

It takes you 3 to 5 business days to respond to a text. That's how we knew Uncrumpled had to be quick.

Sassy

Little one-liners at the iron, or about who we are. Frank Body register. Cheeky, never mean.

Uncrumpled replaces the iron you never use.

Playful

Little jabs at her, because we are her. The play is in the words, not in stunts.

Pick those pants off the floor, smooth the creases, wear them for 3 days straight. Uncrumpled doesn't judge.

Relatable chaos

The real life behind the post. Messy house, 6,000 unread emails, too busy to iron or clean. We never tell her to get it together.

We're here to smooth over life's little wrinkles.

Sound like

  • A mate texting from the car park, not a brand posting.
  • Short sentences. Full stops. One plain statement, then one small shrug.
  • Honest about what it does and what it does not do.
  • First person. Ann started this and Ann is in the copy.
  • Occasionally a Frank Body comparison: "creases drop faster than your Sunday plans".

Never sound like

  • A pun factory. No wrinkle-free wonder, no crease crusader.
  • A lecture. She knows she should iron less. She knows.
  • A science lab. No formula, technology, solution. CreaseDrop is the one exception.
  • A cheerleader. No "you've got this queen", no "besties".
  • Two jokes in one line. If it needs a second image to land, cut one.

The signature moves

Pulled from the deck's feed mock-ups. Each tile is one move we repeat. Under each: how to use it.

The lime bar

Black Zacbel X on a lime bar, dropped straight onto the photo. This is our caption look on statics and carousel covers. Two bars max: a small plain one, then the loud one.

Copy watch-out: "besties" is cheerleader. "Welcome to the club." is the line.

Wordmark up top, product in the mess

UNCRUMPLED small at the top of every static. Product sits in the pile of clothes, never on a plinth. Timing claims like 60 seconds are allowed.

Series title card

Series get a name and an episode number, like a show. Plain line on the small bar, title on the big bar, arrow chip bottom right to say "swipe".

Check with Ann before any dollar figure goes on a card. We do not usually show spend.

People in every frame

Caught, not posed. Friends mid-laugh, someone mid-sentence. The deck's street series (Laundry Confessions: "when did you last touch an iron?") is exactly this.

Anyone recognisable on camera needs a release.

The mess is the set

Real desk, real rollers, real coffee. Shoot the Type C moment as it is, then put one honest line on it. Never tidy the frame.

Spray at position three

Flat lays and packing shots work when the spray is one item among the others, not the hero. Third thing your eye lands on.

Hands, not faces, is fine too

Phone, keys, coffee, spray, all in one hand. Reads as her day. Good for stories and covers when nobody is free to be on camera.

The scale gag

A giant bottle on a trolley across a pedestrian crossing. Funny once a month, not once a week. Sassy, not wacky.

04Captions and hook text

Three reels Ann sent as the reference for how our text on screen should feel. What we take from each, then our seven treatments with a mock of each in our fonts and colours.

Reference 1

https://www.instagram.com/reel/DcozZcaBatK/

I like how there are different fonts and sometimes it is just one word and sometimes it is more. She changes the fonts a bit too much but it looks dynamic and engaging.

Take

  • The rhythm: one word on its own, then a short phrase, then one word again.
  • Two type voices playing off each other, a bold sans and a script.
  • Text sits where the picture has room, not always centred.

Leave

  • Four or five fonts. We use two plus one accent.
  • Pink glow. Our glow, if any, is lime.

Reference 2

https://www.instagram.com/p/Dcby672sHei/

I like how the hook captions are different and a bit more engaging with font, emojis etc.

Take

  • The hook card: one big word, a small italic subline, one emoji.
  • Name chips: a person or thing gets a lime title and a small pill with a role.
  • Screenshot overlays of real messages while she talks.
  • Emphasis words: italic serif lead-in, then one lime word in the display font.
  • Her lime is basically our lime. This is the closest reference to us.

Leave

  • Nothing to leave. Just fewer treatments per video than she uses.

Reference 3

https://www.instagram.com/reel/Ddgv-aUh_0w/

I like how she has some scenes with just text on screen and no image in the background. We would need to use this really sparingly, when saying something important or emphasising.

Take

  • Text-only frames on flat colour for the one line that matters.
  • Word-by-word white captions that follow the voice exactly.
  • A quote stacked word by word down the frame.
  • One word in caps when it is the point (PROFIT).
  • Inset video on a flat colour card, like a photo laid on a table.

Leave

  • Frequency. She cuts to flat frames often. We do it once or twice a video.
  • Pale blue. Ours is paper or pale lime.

The hook card

First frame. One big word or a short phrase in Zacbel X, lime or black. A small subline in italic serif or Zacbel Light. One emoji at most, and only here. The hook says something the audio does not, so the viewer gets two lines in the first second.

Caption rules

Two fonts, one accentZacbel X and Montserrat. Italic serif for lead-ins only. Nothing else, ever.
Three text coloursWhite on video. Black on lime or paper. Lime for the emphasis word. That is the whole list.
One emoji, hook onlyNever in running captions.
Text-only frames: one or twoPer video. For the line that matters. Never the hook.
Lime words: two or threePer video. Never two cards in a row.
Never over a faceCaptions go lower middle or top third. Faces are the point.
Safe zonesKeep text out of the bottom 20% and the right 15%, where Instagram puts its buttons.
The hook is not the first line of audioTwo lines in the first second, not the same line twice.
Copy comes from the voiceEvery caption passes the say-this-not-that table. Sassy, not besties.

06Before you post

Tick at least one. Not all six, one is enough.

Things that are always true

  • Aussie owned. Never Aussie made or made in Australia.
  • Cotton, linen, viscose, rayon and their blends. Not polyester. Silk is yes, patch test an inside seam first.
  • Certified Chaos is parked. Type C and Type C Club are fine.
  • No em dashes. Commas and full stops.
  • Anyone recognisable on camera has signed a release.
  • The spray is never the hero of the frame. Position three.
