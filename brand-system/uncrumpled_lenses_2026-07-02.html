The six lenses,
the Uncrumpled version.

The brands in the scan showed us the six ways to build a feeling. This is each one rebuilt for us: what it means, what it looks like, and a bank of ideas you can shoot. Pull from here whenever the calendar's blank.

Lens 1

Villain

For us
Our enemy isn't a rival spray, it's the whole "supposed-to" life: the tedious chores, the pressure to be perfect, waking early to press a shirt. Ironing is its sharpest symbol.
What it looks like
Juxtapose ironing vs not-ironing, over and over. Put the ironing life next to the living life and let the contrast do the work, in lots of different scenarios. Also paint the iron as the villain through honest comparisons (was 30 minutes even worth it?). Always aim at the behaviour, never the person.
The two lives · lead idea, endless versions
The mechanicShow the ironing life beside the living life, again and again. Same simple format, new scenario each time.
  • "The holiday you want vs the holiday your Nan wants for you."
  • "What I could have been doing instead of ironing."
  • "Your morning with an iron vs your morning with us."
This is the engine of the whole lens. One repeatable idea, unlimited scenarios: any "ironing life vs living life" split works.
Can you even tell? · the honest comparison
Hook"One of these took 30 minutes. Can you even tell which?" Iron a shirt, then spray one. Wear both around the day.
Yes the iron's a touch crisper, but walking round the office, honestly, no one can tell. Compares the time AND quietly asks "was it worth it?"
The supposed-to list
On-screen"Things you're 'supposed' to do that nobody actually cares about:" iron the sheets, own an iron, wake at 6 to press a shirt…
Lands on: "spray it and go live."
Ironing's stealing your Sundays
Hook"Ironing is quietly stealing your Sundays."
The wasting-your-time angle, kept cheeky, never preachy.
Things to leave in 2026
On-screen"Things to leave behind in 2026: people who don't serve you, your 6am alarm, and your iron."
Funny, lumps the iron in with the stuff to ditch.
Don't: knock a competitor, call ironing a "scam," or judge people who iron. The enemy is the chore and the "supposed-to," and the tone is fun, never mean.
Lens 2

Emotion + community

For us
Sell the feeling (the exhale, the weight off your shoulders, permission to not be perfect) and the vibe of a fun, real, adventurous life. The message beneath it all: your life wasn't made for ironing.
What it looks like
Showcase the fun, the mess, the real, the highs and the lows. "Life's a rollercoaster, live it." Show the freedoms ironing's perfectionism costs, not generic lifestyle. (Light on polls for now, we don't have the community yet.)
Done is better than perfect · lead idea
On-screen"A good-enough life beats a perfect one." Someone ironing every garment (bored, stuck) vs someone spraying and walking straight out the door.
The real aspiration: the things ironing's ritual steals, sleeping in, out in seconds, spraying while you watch your show, enjoying the holiday. Not beach/coffee (you can do those with an iron too).
Real-life scenarios · does double duty (emotion + villain)
Rotate these, don't post just oneThe everyday moments where the iron just isn't worth it, then: spray, sorted, out you go.
  • "Meeting in 5 and you catch yourself in the mirror."
  • "You want to wear THAT outfit but can't face the ironing board."
  • "One shirt. Is it really worth pulling out the whole board?"
  • "Putting the shirt back in the wardrobe because it's creased and you can't be bothered."
These are the feeling (relief) AND they paint the iron as the villain, two in one. Every one is a situation people instantly recognise.
Less ironing, more living
ExecutionSomeone tossing the iron aside, cut to the back of a motorbike / a hotel / actually living. "Less ironing, more living."
The identity line over real living. Also carries through the villain content.
What you did instead of ironing
Series · later"We asked what you did instead of ironing. Here's what we got."
The community repost engine, for once customer clips are coming in.
Tag the friend · test
Caption"Tag the friend who irons their bed sheets." (or their curtains)
Keep it about the OTT ironing, light and funny. Never call out normal ironing or make anyone the bad guy.
Lens 3 · untapped

Guilt → pride

For us
Skipping the iron isn't lazy, it's smart and productive. Reframe Uncrumpled as the clever, time-saving, right way to get ready, and subtly signal people can ditch the iron, without preaching or ever implying people who iron are wasting their life.
What it looks like
Nudge, don't force. Frame not-ironing as a smart shortcut you'd be pleased to know, not a cop-out.
This is your sign · the subtle nudge
Hooks (pick one)
  • "This is your sign to never iron again."
  • "Consider this your out."
  • "No one's making you iron. Just saying."
  • "It's here if you ever want to stop."
A gentle nudge, not permission we grant. It's here if you need it, no pressure.
Not lazy, the smart way · lead idea
Hooks (pick one)
  • "It's not lazy. It's optimised."
  • "The ironing hack no one told you about."
  • "The smarter way to get ready."
  • "Save 10 minutes every single morning."
Frame Uncrumpled as productive, a clever time-saving hack, the right way to do it. Not a shortcut to apologise for.
Nobody's looking that closely
Hooks (pick one)
  • "On your deathbed you won't think about the shirts you ironed."
  • "Do you really think your colleagues are studying your shirt that closely?"
  • "Nobody notices a crease as much as you do."
  • "Does anyone actually care if it's perfectly pressed?"
The "no one really cares about the crease" vein. Needs several hooks so it's a proper content idea, not a one-off.
Stopped stressing about
On-screen"Things I've stopped stressing about:" the unmade bed, the takeaway, and ironing.
About us, never judging others. Repeatable with different lists.
Lens 4

Branding

For us
Own the language ("less ironing, more living," "Spray. Smooth. Go.," "sorry Nan") and a warm, real look, so a post is recognisably us even with the logo covered.
What it looks like
Repeat the same lines and end-card every time. Keep the look warm and real (explained below).
Own the language · lead idea
Lines we own"Spray. Smooth. Go." · "Sorry Nan." · "Less ironing, more living." + fun, sassy anti-iron lines: "not today, iron," "iron who?"
Our voice is fun, sassy, energetic, cheeky, never corporate. That tone is part of the brand, use it in every caption.
Locked end-card
Every videoEnd on the same lockup: "Uncrumpled. Less ironing, more living." Same type + colour every time.
Trains recognition post after post.
Signature on-screen text
RuleOne font, one placement, one colour for every hook caption.
Pick it once, never change it.
What "warm and real" actually means
Colours
Warm and cosy, creamy, golden, sunny. A warm filter/grade over the video. Not cool blue, grey, or clinical bright-white.
Settings
Real everyday places, home, kitchen, café, hotel, out and about. Not a sterile studio or a clean product-only shoot.
Light
Natural daylight, soft morning or golden-hour light.
Feel
A bit imperfect and lived-in, like real life. Not staged, shiny or too polished.
"Cold laundry-aisle blue"
The clinical blue-white sterile look of supermarket cleaning-product ads. We are the opposite of that. The moodboard shows the exact feel we want.
Lens 5 · untapped

Build in public

For us
Ann and the journey are the biggest untapped asset. Show building Uncrumpled in the open, the wins, the losses, the scrappy hacks, not showing off the product (that's other content).
What it looks like
Honest, unpolished. A running diary of the journey and how things evolve. Surreal proves the scrappiness itself is the content.
Day X of building Uncrumpled · lead idea
Serial"Day 74 of building Uncrumpled."
A running diary of the journey and how it's evolving. (Exact framing still to lock, but it's about building it, not "making ironing obsolete.")
The founder grenade · incl. budget confessions
Hooks
  • "I spent $400 on a video and it flopped. Here's what I learned."
  • "We couldn't afford a studio, so we filmed this in my kitchen. Please buy a bottle."
Honest wins AND losses, including the scrappy-spend confessions (pure Surreal energy).
Milestone posts
Moment"We just hit our first 1,000 bottles / first retailer, because of you."
Fans feel like co-owners.
Behind the mess
RealAnn packing orders at the kitchen table, unglamorous and honest.
Trust is built here.
The origin story
Hook"I started a company because I hate ironing that much."
The why, told as a hook.
Bring them into decisions · later
Interaction"Help me pick the next scent."
Once we've built more of a community.
Lens 6

Entertainment

For us
Where lenses 1-5 come together. Every post is a bit, not an ad. Once the villain, the emotion, the guilt-flip, the language and Ann are all in play, the content writes itself.
What it looks like
Scrappy, funny, native. You don't need Dr. Squatch's budget, you need Surreal's cheek. Make people laugh or feel something, then smuggle the sell inside.
The two holidays
On-screen"The holiday Nan thinks I had vs the holiday I had."
Prim expectation, then the real, fun version.
Less of this, more of this
StructureThe ironing life, hard cut to the good life. Fast, on a track.
Organic post and paid ad.
Cool Nan
On-screen"What Nan's supposed to be doing" (ironing, high tea) → "what Nan's actually doing" (sprays a shirt, rides off on a motorbike).
Flip the stereotype.
Deadpan "can't be bothered"
Voice"We can't be bothered ironing either. That's kind of the whole point."
Surreal energy.
Trend-jack
FormatPut the anti-ironing spin on the week's trending audio or format.
Ride existing reach (check the watch-list).
Things your holiday doesn't need
On-screen"Things your holiday does NOT need: an iron, a 6am alarm, a lecture from your mother."
Cheeky list, lands on the bottle.
Two tests before anything posts

1. The 2-second test (the hook): does the first 2 seconds make someone need to know what happens? If they can nod and scroll, rewrite the hook.

2. The watch test (the whole post): if this wasn't our brand, would you stop and watch it, and would you send it to a friend? If it isn't fun, relatable, surprising or satisfying, it's an ad, not entertainment. Rework it.

(A proper demo / "torture test" and a mock-epic ad are parked for later, not now.)
