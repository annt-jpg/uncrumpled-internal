### Where the demand actually sits

## Miscategorisation note (the "wrinkle" risk)

**Verdict: clothing-specific terms are CLEAN. The skincare collision does NOT bleed into these queries.**

- Every seed and suggestion that contains a fabric/clothing qualifier ("...spray **for clothes**", "wrinkle **release** spray", "**crease** spray", "anti wrinkle spray for clothes") returns **only laundry/fabric SERP results** — Coles/Woolworths/Downy/Lenor, ironing aids, fabric care. Zero anti-ageing/cosmetic results observed on the two live SERPs checked.
- DataForSEO category codes confirm it: clothing terms map to **10010/10081/10423/11595/12829 (apparel/fabric-care/home)** and 10005/10047/10359 (laundry). They do NOT carry the cosmetics/skincare category tree. "Wrinkle release spray" intent = transactional fabric care.
- **The one term to treat with caution = bare "anti wrinkle spray"** (no "for clothes"). Without the fabric qualifier this risks pulling face/Botox-adjacent skincare intent. **Always qualify with "for clothes / clothing / fabric / crease" in targeting, copy, page titles, and hashtags.**
- Practical rule for ad + organic copy: **lead with "wrinkle release spray" or "anti-crease clothing spray" (qualified)**, never naked "anti wrinkle spray." "Crease" is a useful disambiguator (UK/AU FMCG brands like Lenor use "crease releaser") and carries zero skincare risk.
