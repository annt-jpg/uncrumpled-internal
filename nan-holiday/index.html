And the one you're actually having.

Two acts. Ironing before dinner, high tea, lights out at nine. Then dancing, the same dress going out a second night, and two in the morning with your shoes in your hand. The joke is that both holidays are true.

Less ironing. More living.

The locked line, and it does double duty here: it is the punchline of the joke and the brand line at the same time. Nan does not need to know.

Not a split screen, and not a side by side. Comparison framing is a killed format for this brand, because it always ends up arguing that one person is better than another. Here the contrast lives in the cut and in the words. Act one runs, then act two runs. Nothing is ever divided down the middle of the frame.
Two deliberate reversals from the other films. An iron is shown, but only in act one, where it is the joke rather than the villain. And the product does appear, once, in act two, sitting in the middle of the act and never as the reveal, which is the locked rule about where the spray goes in a list.
Same dress across all three act two frames. That is what makes the middle frame make sense: it is the same green slip she danced in, going out again on the second night. Lose the continuity and the spray frame becomes a product shot instead of a story beat.
Nan is never the butt of it. Act one is genuinely lovely and shot as beautifully as act two. If it reads as mocking a nice holiday the film turns mean, and the brand has never been mean about anybody. Nothing is wrong with high tea. It just is not what happened.

The feel, same as the other films

Watch it. The words here are doing far less work than the look is. It needs to be beautiful and it needs to look expensive. That is the whole reference.

Reference · the texture

Heels carried in the lift, brass taps, earrings at the mirror. Expensive, and everybody in it looks beautiful. That is the bar. The good clothes are treated as a character rather than a prop, the light does most of the work, and it holds every shot well past the point a reel normally cuts.
